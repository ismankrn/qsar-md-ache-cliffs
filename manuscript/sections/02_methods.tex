\section{Methods}

In this section, we introduce the procedures to construct and to evaluate the
descriptor sets compared in this work. We first describe the construction of the
activity-cliff dataset together with the splits on which everything was scored, the
three framings in which every descriptor set was scored, and the four descriptor sets
themselves. We then describe the two procedures that produce the more expensive of
those sets, i.e. the receptor model with its docking and validation, and the molecular
dynamics simulations,
followed by the descriptors derived from the trajectories and the model suite on which
all of the sets were scored.

\subsection{Dataset, activity-cliff definition and splits}\label{sec:dataset}

The dataset was constructed in four steps, i.e. curation of the activity data,
definition of the activity cliffs, selection of the target, and assembly of the pairs
followed by their division into a development and a sealed test part. The curation and
the cliff criterion are described first, because the target was selected by applying
both of them to six candidates.

Activity data were retrieved from ChEMBL for each candidate target, retaining a record
only when it came from a binding assay, reported IC\textsubscript{50}, $K_\mathrm{i}$
or $K_\mathrm{d}$ with an exact relation in nanomolar units, carried a pChEMBL value,
was free of ChEMBL's own data-validity flags and was not marked a potential duplicate.
A single assay type was then kept, the most frequent one, which is
IC\textsubscript{50} for acetylcholinesterase, since a pair formed from an
IC\textsubscript{50} and a $K_\mathrm{i}$ measurement can satisfy a cliff criterion by
virtue of the assay rather than of the chemistry. Structures were standardised by
stripping salts, keeping the largest covalent fragment and neutralising formal charges
from the deposited representation, and those below six heavy atoms were removed.
Replicates of one standardised structure were collapsed to their median, and a
structure whose replicates disagreed by more than one log unit in standard deviation
was discarded rather than averaged, since a compound measured inconsistently can enter
a cliff pair on its own measurement error. For acetylcholinesterase the curated set
holds 5{,}609 compounds spanning pChEMBL 4.00 to 10.96.

Similarity was computed as the Tanimoto coefficient on binary ECFP4, i.e. Morgan
fingerprints of radius 2 folded to 2{,}048 bits. A pair was treated as near-identical
at a coefficient of at least 0.90 and as an activity cliff when its two members also
differed by at least 2.0 log units in pChEMBL, a hundredfold difference in potency.
Pairs were further divided according to whether their two members differ in
constitution or only in stereochemistry, which was decided by comparing their
canonical SMILES with the stereochemistry stripped, and only the constitutional pairs
were treated as informative, since no stereo-blind fingerprint can resolve a
stereoisomer pair by construction while any method that uses three-dimensional
coordinates resolves it trivially. Within the curated acetylcholinesterase set 421
pairs cleared the similarity criterion with a difference of at least one log unit, 110
of them reached the hundredfold criterion and 100 of those were constitutional.

Six candidate targets were screened against three entry criteria, namely at least 80
compounds surviving curation, a potency range of at least 3 log units, and at least 25
constitutional activity cliffs. All six satisfied the first two criteria and two of
them satisfied the third. Acetylcholinesterase was selected from those two because its
constitutional pairs dominate most strongly and because its binding site gives a
mechanistic reason to expect its cliffs to be geometric in origin, since its two
recognition sites sit at opposite ends of a narrow gorge and its inhibitors are
designed to span them, as described in Section~\ref{sec:dock}. The enzyme is also a
therapeutic target in Alzheimer's disease, and the counts behind the choice are
reported in Section~\ref{sec:composition}.

The pool of the selected target was then filtered for simulation feasibility,
retaining
a compound only when it was composed of the elements H, C, N, O, F, S, Cl, Br, I and
P, carried no radical electrons, had a molecular weight between 150 and 700~Da, had at
most 55 heavy atoms and at most 18 rotatable bonds, and could be embedded in three
dimensions by ETKDGv3. This removed 239 of the 5{,}609 compounds, most of them on
molecular weight or on rotatable-bond count. It was applied to the whole pool before
any pairing rather than to the pairs afterwards, because a pair is lost as soon as
either of its members is removed, so that filtering first keeps every surviving pair
intact by construction. Of the 100 constitutional cliff pairs, 97 survived with both
members intact, formed from 99 distinct compounds.

A control pair was near-identical under the same floor of 0.90 but differed by at most
0.30 log units, i.e. less than twofold, so that the two groups differ in the property
under test and in as little else as possible. Stereoisomer pairs were excluded on the
same grounds, and controls were drawn to reproduce the joint distribution of
similarity and mean potency of the cliff pairs over four similarity and five potency
bins, with each control compound used once. This yielded 86 control pairs from 172
compounds, matched closely on both quantities, since the median Tanimoto coefficient
is 1.000 for the cliff pairs against 0.993 for the controls and the mean pair-mean
potency 6.64 against 6.58, while the quantity under test differs by construction at
2.66 log units against 0.13. The final set holds 271 ligands and 183 pairs, i.e. 97
cliff and 86 control. A ligand was prepared and simulated once while a pair refers to
two ligands, since a compound may belong to more than one pair. The dominant
protonation state at pH 7.4 was assigned by explicit rules, and every ligand carries a
net positive charge, 170 at $+1$ and 101 at $+2$, consistent with the cation--$\pi$
recognition at the aromatic residues lining the gorge.

This set was then divided into a development and a test part, once and in a separate
step that wrote the assignment to a file, with the random seed being 20260902.
Recomputing the folds inside each model would have let the comparison measure the luck
of the fold as much as the quality of the descriptors, and fixing them before any
molecular-dynamics result existed removed the possibility of adjusting them, knowingly
or otherwise, in a direction that flatters a conclusion. Splitting at the level of the
ligand would have been incorrect, since each ligand
belongs to at least one pair and placing one member of a cliff pair in the training
set and the other in the test set would allow a model to memorise the shared scaffold.
The splits were therefore built from the connected components of the graph whose
vertices are the ligands and whose edges are the pairs, a component being the smallest
unit that can be moved without separating a pair. The 271 ligands formed 110 such
components of between 2 and 9 ligands, with a median of 2. The components were
stratified by whether they contained a cliff pair and by their mean potency in three
bins, and 20 per cent of each stratum was assigned to the test split. The remainder
were distributed over five cross-validation folds by greedy bin packing, taking the
largest components first and assigning each to the fold with the lowest combined load
of cliff pairs and of ligands, since balancing on either quantity alone had given
folds of 25 cliff pairs against 4 or of 13 ligands against 63.

This gave a development split of 217 ligands and 145 pairs and a test split of 54
ligands and 38 pairs, 20 of which are cliff pairs, with the five folds holding between
40 and 45 ligands, between 15 and 17 cliff pairs and between 13 and 14 control pairs.
No pair has its two members on opposite sides of the test boundary or of any fold
boundary, which was verified on the written splits rather than assumed from the
construction. The test split was sealed, i.e. it was to be scored only after a single
descriptor set and a single algorithm had been selected on the development split,
since with several descriptor sets and six algorithm families a winner can be selected
on noise alone. No model reported in this work has been scored on it, that condition
was not met, and the split therefore remains unused.

\subsection{Three framings of the prediction problem}\label{sec:framings}

    $\mathbf{d}_b$ of the two ligands of a pair, the metric, and the number of

  \input{../results/31_framings.tex}
\end{table}

A comparison between descriptor sets has no single answer, since what counts as an
improvement depends on what is being predicted. A descriptor set can predict potency
well and still fail to tell two near-identical molecules apart, and a set that
separates cliff pairs from control pairs need not predict the size of the difference
within a pair. Three framings were therefore scored for every descriptor set, on the
folds of Section~\ref{sec:dataset} and with the same algorithms, so that the three
results differ in the question being asked and in nothing else, and they are set out
in Table~\ref{tab:framings}. Framing~B was designated the primary comparison before
any molecular-dynamics descriptor had been scored, and that designation is recorded in
the version history of the analysis code rather than asserted after the fact.

Framing~A is ordinary potency prediction, i.e. the task on which the QSAR literature
is calibrated. It was included because a descriptor set that cannot predict potency at
all is no candidate for predicting a difference in potency, and because its result can
be placed beside published models in a way that a pairwise result cannot. Framing~B
predicts the signed difference within a pair, which is the quantity that defines an
activity cliff, and each pair contributes two rows with the difference taken in both
orders, so that the arbitrary choice of which member is written first cannot influence
what the model learns. It is the primary framing because the question of this study is
whether a descriptor set can distinguish two molecules that a fingerprint declares
identical, and that distinction is the signed difference within a pair and nothing
else.

Framing~C classifies a pair as a cliff or a matched control. Its features have to be
invariant to the order of the two members, since the label is, and the antisymmetric
rows of framing~B would leave it ambiguous, so a pair is described by the elementwise
sum and the elementwise absolute difference concatenated. It uses five algorithm
families rather than six, since partial least squares regression has no classification
counterpart. In both pair framings a pair is indivisible, its rows assigned to the
same fold and resampled together, and the pair is also the unit of the intervals of
Section~\ref{sec:eval}, since treating its two rows as independent would count every
pair twice and narrow every interval for no reason that the data supports.

\subsection{Four descriptor sets of increasing computational
cost}\label{sec:rungs}

\begin{table}[tb]
  \centering
  \small

    what its descriptors capture, the number of features it was scored with, and the
    cost of producing it for one ligand. The feature counts are the two feature sets
    of rung~1 and, for rung~4, the full set and the set that survives the reliability
    filter. The costs are median measured values for one ligand, given as
    processor time for the first three rungs and as wall-clock time on one GPU for
    the simulation, which are not the same unit and are not comparable beyond their
    order of magnitude.}
  \label{tab:rungs}
  \input{../results/32_rungs.tex}
\end{table}

Four descriptor families were compared, ordered by the cost of producing them, and
each is referred to below as a rung of that ladder. The first needs only the molecular
graph, the second a docked pose, the third an energy evaluation on that pose and the
fourth a molecular-dynamics trajectory. The ordering is the point of the comparison,
since the question is not whether a more expensive representation can be built but
whether it buys anything, so every rung was scored with the same algorithms, on the
same folds and in the three framings of Section~\ref{sec:framings}, and a difference
between two rungs is a difference between representations rather than between model
families. Table~\ref{tab:rungs} gives the input each rung requires, what its
descriptors capture, how many features it carries and what it cost.

Rung~1 uses no three-dimensional information. Two feature sets were carried, i.e. the
binary ECFP4 that defined the cliffs in Section~\ref{sec:dataset} and a count-based
ECFP4 of the same width combined with the 217 physicochemical descriptors of RDKit,
and the better of the two was taken in every framing, because neither dominates the
other and scoring only one would have handicapped the baseline against which every
other rung was measured, in the direction that flatters the more expensive rungs.

The rung-2 descriptors were chosen to test one mechanism rather than to cast a wide
net, namely that two closely related inhibitors are distinguished by how persistently
each of them engages the two ends of the gorge and by its position along the axis
between them. A fixed panel of fourteen gorge-lining residues, i.e. residues 72, 74,
86, 121, 124, 203, 286, 295, 297, 334, 337, 338, 341 and 447, is used from here on, so
that the pose-geometry, MM/GBSA and molecular-dynamics descriptors are computed on the
same positions. Each rung-2 descriptor is the median over the three docking seeds of
Section~\ref{sec:dock}, with the spread retained for diagnosis but not used as a
feature.
The heavy-atom count is among them, which is worth stating plainly, since the two
groups of pairs differ in size as reported in Section~\ref{sec:composition} and this
rung is therefore able to use that difference. The same descriptors were also computed
over an ensemble of receptor conformations and are reported separately as rung~2e,
because what limits the docking is the rigidity of a single receptor rather than the
protocol, which is established in Section~\ref{sec:dock}. Eight structures, i.e. 4EY4, 4EY7,
4M0F, 6CQX, 6CQY, 6O4X, 6O5V and 6WUY, were selected by clustering on the side-chain
positions of the gorge-lining residues after superposition, since selecting on
resolution or on date of deposition would most likely have returned several copies of
one conformation. Each descriptor is the median across the eight, and the five axis
descriptors are dropped, being defined against the single 4EY7 geometry.

Rung~3 is single-trajectory, i.e. the complex, the protein and the ligand were taken
from the same coordinates with no re-minimisation after separation, so that the
internal bonded terms cancel exactly and only the intermolecular interaction and the
desolvation remain, and the protein term, which is identical for every ligand under a
rigid receptor, was computed once. It is not a rigorous binding free energy and is not
presented as one, since absolute values obtained from a single post-docking snapshot
are not defensible. It is on the ladder because it is what a structure-based study
routinely does at this cost, and the question is whether that routine step buys
anything.

Rung~4 is described in Sections~\ref{sec:md} and \ref{sec:mddesc}. Twelve of its 33
descriptors survived the reliability filter of Section~\ref{sec:filter}, which removes
those whose variation between repeated simulations of one ligand is comparable to
their variation between ligands, and both sets were scored, since reporting only one
would leave open whether a result is a property of the descriptors or of the filter.
The number of features is not matched across the rungs, deliberately, because each was
scored in the form in which it would actually be used and a comparison that first cut
a fingerprint down to 34 features would describe nothing that anyone does. A
difference between two rungs is therefore a difference between representations at
their natural dimensionality, and a matched-dimensionality arm was scored as well, as
a robustness check, with the feature selection performed inside each training fold so
that the held-out fold can never influence which features are chosen.

\subsection{Receptor model, docking protocol and its
validation}\label{sec:dock}

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Figure1_hAChE_structure.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Human acetylcholinesterase and its catalytic gorge (PDB 4EY7).}
    (A)~Overall fold, rainbow-coloured from N- to C-terminus; donepezil in the
    crystal pose marks the gorge as black spheres. (B)~The architecture of the gorge, formed by Trp86 at
    the catalytic anionic site, Trp286 at the peripheral anionic site, the swinging
    Tyr337 gate between them, and the Ser203--His447--Glu334 catalytic triad.}
  \label{fig:structure}
\end{figure}

The receptor model was prepared from the X-ray crystal structure of recombinant hAChE
in complex with donepezil, with the PDB ID being 4EY7 \citep{cheung2012} and the
resolution being \SI{2.35}{\angstrom}. Chain~A was retained and the second copy in the
asymmetric unit was discarded. The enzyme is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:structure}(A),
where donepezil in its crystal pose marks the active site at the base of a narrow
gorge, and the architecture of that gorge in Figure~\ref{fig:structure}(B), with an
aromatic residue at each end, i.e. Trp86 at the catalytic anionic site (CAS) and
Trp286 at the peripheral anionic site (PAS) near the mouth, and with Tyr337 between
the two, which adopts different rotamers in different complexes and is therefore
referred to as the gate. In this complex the CZ3 atoms of the two indole rings were
measured \SI{15.6}{\angstrom} apart, and donepezil spans that distance while
contacting both residues.

Donepezil is itself a dual-binding-site inhibitor, which is why this structure was
chosen. Inhibitors designed to span both ends of the gorge are an established design
principle rather than a premise introduced here \citep{grabowska2025}, since the
peripheral anionic site has been reported to accelerate the aggregation of
$\beta$-amyloid alongside the catalytic function of the enzyme, and it is this
principle that the descriptor sets of Section~\ref{sec:rungs} were constructed to
test. Preparation of the same receptor for simulation differs and is described in
Section~\ref{sec:md}, since it becomes relevant only once a force field is applied.

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Figure2_redocking_validation.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Redocking validation, and the limit of a rigid receptor.}
    (A)~Donepezil redocked into its own structure reproduces the crystal pose to
    0.75~\AA{} RMSD over five seeds. (B)~Huperzine~A docked into its own receptor
    (4EY5) succeeds. (C)~The same ligand cross-docked into 4EY7 fails at
    3.10~\AA{}, because the Tyr337 rotamer of this structure occludes the true
    pose.}
  \label{fig:redock}
\end{figure}

All ligands were docked into the prepared 4EY7 receptor by using AutoDock Vina
\citep{vina}. The search box was a cube of \SI{30}{\angstrom} per side centred at
$(-14.257, -42.544, 30.646)$~\AA{}, midway between the CAS and the PAS so that poses
spanning the gorge were accessible, and the exhaustiveness was set to 32. Ligand
structures were generated from the standardised SMILES by adding hydrogens, embedding
a single conformer with the ETKDGv3 method under a fixed random seed and optimising it
with the MMFF94 force field, all by using RDKit \citep{rdkit}, and were converted to
the PDBQT format by using Meeko \citep{meeko}. Each ligand was docked with three
independent seeds, and the pose-geometry descriptors were computed on the top-ranked
pose of each seed and reduced to their median, with the spread across seeds retained
so that unstable poses could be identified.

The protocol was validated by redocking four ligands whose complexes with hAChE have
been solved, with five seeds per ligand and a pose counted as successful below
\SI{2.0}{\angstrom} RMSD from the crystal pose. Donepezil was reproduced at
$0.745 \pm 0.009$~\AA{} in five of five runs, as shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:redock}(A), and territrem~B at $0.740 \pm 0.012$~\AA{} in five of
five. Galantamine gave $2.463 \pm 1.709$~\AA{} and succeeded in four of five, and
huperzine~A gave $3.098 \pm 0.011$~\AA{} and failed in all five. That failure was
systematic rather than stochastic, since the spread across seeds was
\SI{0.011}{\angstrom}, and two explanations were tested. Repeating the docking with
boxes of 18, 22, 26 and \SI{30}{\angstrom} per side left the RMSD of huperzine~A
between 3.05 and \SI{3.18}{\angstrom}, so box size was not the cause. Docking each
ligand into the structure of its own complex instead succeeded, with huperzine~A at
\SI{0.23}{\angstrom} in 4EY5, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:redock}(B), and galantamine
at \SI{0.81}{\angstrom} in 4EY6, while the same ligand and the same protocol failed in
4EY7, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:redock}(C), so that the two panels differ only in
the receptor. The Tyr337 side chain differs by \SI{2.53}{\angstrom} between 4EY5 and
4EY7 and occludes the true huperzine~A pose in the latter. The limitation is therefore
the rigidity of the receptor rather than the docking protocol, which is the reason for
the ensemble docking of Section~\ref{sec:rungs} and, ultimately, for the molecular
dynamics of Section~\ref{sec:md}. Vina also proved strongly stochastic for small ligands, since
galantamine gave 5.88 and \SI{1.60}{\angstrom} on different seeds under otherwise
identical settings, which is why seeds were fixed and three replicates were run for
every ligand.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_interactions.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{The reference binding mode, and docking checked at the level of

  \label{fig:interactions}

Since the pose-geometry and molecular-dynamics descriptors are defined in terms of
residue contacts rather than RMSD, the protocol was validated a second time at the
level of interactions. Interaction fingerprints were computed for the crystal pose and
the redocked pose of donepezil by using ProLIF \citep{prolif2021}, with bond orders
transferred from the docked structure so that the two poses carry identical chemistry
and differ only in their coordinates. The crystal binding mode is shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:interactions}(a) and the comparison between the two fingerprints in
Figure~\ref{fig:interactions}(b). Six residues are contacted in both poses, five in
the crystal pose only and two in the docked pose only, and every typed interaction at
the three named sites is recovered, i.e. the hydrophobic and $\pi$-stacking contacts
at Trp86 and at Trp286 and the cation--$\pi$ and hydrophobic contacts at Tyr337, while
what is lost on docking is van der Waals contact alone with Ser203 and His447. The
typing was used for validation only and enters no descriptor set.

\subsection{Molecular dynamics}\label{sec:md}

The receptor used for simulation is not the one used for docking. The crystal
structure does not resolve residues 259 to 264 and 495 to 497, and the force-field
setup treated each chain as continuous and bridged both gaps with peptide bonds of
7.11 and \SI{4.05}{\angstrom}, which carried 166{,}885 of the system's
185{,}963~\si{\kilo\joule\per\mole} of bond energy, so that a minimisation would have
pulled the flanking segments together and deformed the fold. Rebuilding the loops was
attempted and abandoned, since the best of four attempts gave four non-proline cis
peptide bonds in one loop of six residues against a natural rate near 0.03 per cent.
What had to go was the fabricated bond rather than the absence of the loop, so the
chain was divided at the breaks into the segments 4--258, 265--494 and 498--541, each
given its own chain identifier and capped with an acetyl group at its N-terminal and
an N-methylamide group at its C-terminal side. The caps are neutral rather than
charged termini, which would have added six artificial formal charges on the surface,
and were placed by internal coordinates, so that the procedure is deterministic. Bond
energy fell to 1{,}145~\si{\kilo\joule\per\mole} and the maximum force from
$3.3\times10^{8}$ to $2.1\times10^{5}$~\si{\kilo\joule\per\mole\per\nano\metre},
while the atoms lining the gorge did not move at all, so that the docked poses remain
valid and nothing earlier in this work had to be repeated.

    are the minimum and maximum across systems; a single value means every system
    shares it.}

One system was built per ligand rather than per trajectory, so that a replicate
differs from its parent only in the velocity seed and therefore tests the sampling
rather than the structure. The protein was described by the amber14SB force field, the
ligand by OpenFF Sage 2.3.0 through a SMIRNOFF template generator and the solvent by
the TIP3P water model with sodium chloride at \SI{0.15}{\mole\per\litre}, which is the
combination used for the MM/GBSA calculation of Section~\ref{sec:rungs}, since a
difference there would have turned a comparison of rungs into a comparison of force
fields. Each ligand was placed in its docked pose in a cubic box of
\SI{100.1}{\angstrom} per side, giving between 96{,}220 and 96{,}298 atoms, with the
composition reported in Table~\ref{tab:system}. Two quantities were checked for every
system rather than assumed, i.e. the net charge after counterions, which is at most
$2.8\times10^{-13}\,e$ across all 217 systems, and the shortest receptor--ligand
heavy-atom distance at build time, which runs from 1.67 to \SI{2.64}{\angstrom}. The
lower end of that range is a tight contact and is stated rather than glossed, since
what resolves it is the minimisation and equilibration that follow.

Each system was energy-minimised by steepest descent to a maximum force of
\SI{100}{\kilo\joule\per\mole\per\nano\metre}, equilibrated for \SI{100}{\pico\second}
in the canonical ensemble with the solute position-restrained and for a further
\SI{100}{\pico\second} in the isothermal-isobaric ensemble without restraints.
Production used a \SI{2}{\femto\second} time step with the bonds to hydrogen
constrained by LINCS, a Verlet neighbour list, particle-mesh Ewald electrostatics, a
\SI{1.0}{\nano\metre} cutoff for both the real-space Coulomb and the van der Waals
interactions with a long-range dispersion correction applied to the energy and the
pressure, the velocity-rescaling thermostat at \SI{300}{\kelvin} with a coupling time
of \SI{0.1}{\pico\second}, and the c-rescale barostat at \SI{1}{\bar}, isotropic, with
a coupling time of \SI{2}{\pico\second} and a compressibility of
$4.5\times10^{-5}$~\si{\per\bar}. Coordinates were written every
\SI{10}{\pico\second}.

The simulations were run with the GPU-resident mdrun of GROMACS, submitted in batches
through its multi-directory interface with a wall-clock limit per job and restarts
taken from checkpoints. 229 production runs were completed over the 217 ligands, 211
of them simulated once and 6 three times for the replicate pilot of
Section~\ref{sec:filter}, amounting to 6{,}965~ns. The production length was
\SI{50}{\nano\second} for the first 56 runs and \SI{25}{\nano\second} thereafter, and
17 of those 56 stopped between 34 and \SI{42}{\nano\second} on reaching their
wall-clock limit. The mixture of lengths does not affect the descriptors, since the
first \SI{5}{\nano\second} of every run was discarded and the descriptors were
computed on the \SI{20}{\nano\second} that follows, which every run supplies in full.
