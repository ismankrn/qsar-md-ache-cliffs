\section{Methods}

\subsection{Dataset, activity-cliff definition and data splitting}\label{sec:dataset}

This work is concerned with activity cliffs, i.e. pairs of compounds that are
near-identical in structure and yet far apart in potency. All of the bioactivity data
used here were taken from ChEMBL \citep{chembl}, which records measured potencies
against individual protein targets. The target whose
cliffs are studied here is human acetylcholinesterase (hAChE), with the ChEMBL target
identifier being CHEMBL220. The
enzyme hydrolyses acetylcholine, and its inhibition raises the synaptic concentration
of this neurotransmitter, which is the basis of the cholinergic treatment of
Alzheimer's disease. The hAChE target was selected because it met three entry criteria,
namely at least 80 compounds surviving curation, a potency range of at least 3 log
units, and at least 25 constitutional activity cliffs. 

Both halves of the cliff definition need a threshold, one for how alike the two
members are and one for how far apart their potencies lie. Similarity was computed as
the Tanimoto coefficient on binary ECFP4, i.e. Morgan fingerprints of radius 2 folded
to 2{,}048 bits, and a pair was treated as near-identical at a coefficient of at least
0.90. It was treated as a cliff when its two members also differed by at least 2.0 log
units in pChEMBL, which is a hundredfold difference in potency. Pairs were further
divided according to whether their two members differ in constitution or only in
stereochemistry, which was decided by comparing their canonical SMILES with the
stereochemistry stripped, and only the constitutional pairs were treated as
informative. ECFP is blind to stereochemistry, so a stereo-blind fingerprint cannot
resolve a stereoisomer pair at all, while any method that uses three-dimensional
coordinates resolves it trivially.

The dataset itself was then built in three steps, i.e. the curation of the activity
data, the identification of the cliff pairs, and the assembly of the pairs followed by
their splitting into a development and a sealed test set. The activity data of hAChE
were retrieved first, and only records from binding assays were kept.
A record also had to report IC\textsubscript{50}, $K_\mathrm{i}$ or $K_\mathrm{d}$
with an exact relation in nanomolar units, to carry a pChEMBL value, to be free of
ChEMBL's own data-validity flags and not to be marked a potential duplicate. A single
assay type was then kept, the most frequent one, which is IC\textsubscript{50} here.
Mixing assay types is unsafe, as a pair formed from an IC\textsubscript{50} and a
$K_\mathrm{i}$ measurement can satisfy a cliff criterion by virtue of the assay alone.
Structures were standardised by stripping salts, keeping the largest covalent fragment
and neutralising formal charges from the deposited representation, and those below six
heavy atoms were removed. Replicate measurements of one standardised structure were
collapsed to their median, and a structure whose replicates disagreed by more than one
log unit in standard deviation was discarded instead of being averaged. A compound
measured inconsistently can otherwise enter a cliff pair on the strength of its own
measurement error. The curated set holds 5{,}609 compounds spanning pChEMBL 4.00 to
10.96, within which 421 pairs cleared the similarity criterion with a difference of at
least one log unit, 110 of them reached the hundredfold criterion, and 100 of those
were constitutional.

The compound pool was then filtered for simulation feasibility, and a compound was
kept only when it met five conditions. It had to be composed of the elements H, C, N, O, F, S, Cl, Br, I and P, and to carry
no radical electrons. It also had to have a molecular weight between 150 and 700~Da,
at most 55 heavy atoms and at most 18 rotatable bonds, and to be embeddable in three
dimensions by ETKDGv3. This removed 239 of the 5{,}609
compounds, most of them on molecular weight or on rotatable-bond count. We applied the
filter to the whole pool before any pairing, because a pair is lost as soon as either
of its members is removed, so that filtering first keeps every surviving pair intact
by construction. Of the 100 constitutional
cliff pairs, 97 survived with both members intact, formed from 99 distinct compounds.

A control pair was near-identical under the same floor of 0.90, but its two members
differed by at most 0.30 log units, less than twofold. The two groups then differ in
the property under test and in as little else as possible. Stereoisomer pairs were excluded here on the same grounds, and the controls were drawn to reproduce the
joint distribution of similarity and mean potency of the cliff pairs over four
similarity and five potency bins, using each control compound once. The procedure
yielded 86 control pairs from 172 compounds, and the matching is close on both
quantities. The median Tanimoto coefficient is 1.000 for the cliff pairs against 0.993
for the controls, and the mean pair-mean potency 6.64 against 6.58, while the quantity
under test differs by construction at 2.66 log units against 0.13. The final set holds
271 ligands and 183 pairs, 97 cliff and 86 control. Each ligand was prepared and simulated once, while a pair refers
to two ligands, as a compound may belong to more than one pair. Each ligand was prepared in two forms, of which the standardised neutral structure
carries the fingerprints and the pair definition. The dominant protonation state
at pH 7.4, assigned by explicit rules, is the one that enters the docking and the
simulations. Every ligand
carries a net positive charge in that second form, 170 at $+1$ and 101 at $+2$.

Every cliff pair in the final set is constitutional, as the two members differ in
molecular formula in all 97 and the smallest difference in heavy-atom count is one. No
result in this work therefore rests on a pair that a stereo-blind fingerprint is barred
from resolving. The fingerprint nevertheless fails to separate pairs that did enter the set.
Binary ECFP4 reported a Tanimoto coefficient of exactly 1.000 for 49 of the 97 cliff
pairs, whose two members differ by a median of 4 and by as many as 8 heavy atoms, and
in potency by a median of 2.51 log units. Count-based ECFP4 separated those same 49
pairs at a median coefficient of 0.878, as Figure~\ref{fig:dataset}(a) shows, so
folding counts to bits discards the multiplicity that distinguishes a molecule from its
longer homologue. This bears on the selection and not only on the descriptors, since
the similarity recorded at selection agrees exactly with the audited binary coefficient
over all 97 pairs. Figure~\ref{fig:dataset}(b) places the pairs in the plane of the
definition, where the empty band between the cliff pairs above 2.0 log units and the
controls below 0.30 is imposed by the criterion rather than by the chemistry. The two
groups also differ in size, by 4.4 heavy atoms on average within a cliff pair against
1.8 within a control, which Figure~\ref{fig:dataset}(c) shows and which
Section~\ref{sec:eval} adjusts for.

This set was then divided once into a development and a test part, with the random
seed being 20260902, and was never revised afterwards. The unit of assignment is not the ligand but the connected component of the graph
whose vertices are the ligands and whose edges are the pairs. Every ligand belongs to
at least one pair, and a split that separated a pair would let a model memorise the
shared scaffold from the training side, so the 271 ligands were handled as the 110
components that they form. These were stratified by whether they contain a cliff pair
and by their mean potency, and 20 per cent of each stratum was assigned to the test
split. The remainder were distributed over five cross-validation folds balanced
jointly on cliff pairs and on ligand count, since either quantity alone leaves the
other badly skewed. Table~\ref{tab:splits}
gives the result, and no pair has its two members on opposite sides of the test
boundary or of any fold boundary, which was verified on the written splits. The test
split was to be opened only once a single descriptor set and algorithm had been
selected, which did not happen, so no model reported in this work has been scored on
it.

\subsection{Three framings of the prediction problem}\label{sec:framings}

A comparison between descriptor sets has no single answer, as what counts as an
improvement depends on what is being predicted. Activity cliffs are the standard
counter-example to QSAR for exactly this reason, since models that predict potency
well across a series are reported to fail on the near-identical pairs within it
\citep{vantilborg2022, dablander2023}. The three framings are different functions of the same descriptors, so a result on one does not determine a result on
another, and separating cliff pairs from control pairs does not require predicting the
size of the difference within a pair. Three framings were therefore scored for every
descriptor set, on the folds of Section~\ref{sec:dataset} and with the same algorithms, which are
described in Section~\ref{sec:algo}, so that the three results differ in the question
being asked and in nothing else. Table~\ref{tab:framings} sets them side by side, with $\mathbf{d}_a$ and
$\mathbf{d}_b$ being the descriptor vectors of the two ligands of a pair and the
number in parentheses the count of units available in the development split.

    the features of one row, and the metric.}
  \label{tab:framings}
  \input{../results/31_framings.tex}
\end{table}

In framing~A the unit is the ligand, the target is its pChEMBL value, and the metric
is the coefficient of determination over the out-of-fold predictions. The predictions of the five folds are pooled before the metric is computed, so a fold
that happens to be easy cannot be averaged against one that is hard. This is ordinary
potency prediction, the task on which the QSAR literature is calibrated, and we
included it for two reasons. A descriptor set that cannot predict potency at all is no
candidate for predicting a difference in potency. A result on this task can also be
placed beside published models in a way that a pairwise result cannot.

In framing~B the unit is the pair and the target is the signed difference in pChEMBL between its two members, which is the
quantity that defines an activity cliff, scored again by the coefficient of
determination. Each pair contributes two rows, with the
difference between the descriptor vectors taken in both orders, so that the arbitrary
choice of which member comes first cannot influence what the model learns. The construction also makes the failure mode explicit, since a pair whose two members
carry identical descriptor vectors yields a row of zeros and any model must then
predict a difference of zero. Framing~B is the primary comparison of this work, as the
question being asked is whether a descriptor set can distinguish two molecules that a
fingerprint declares identical. That distinction is the signed difference within a
pair and nothing else.

In framing~C the unit is again the pair, but the target is categorical, namely whether
the pair is a cliff or a matched control, and the metric is the area under the
receiver operating characteristic curve. The features of a pair have to be invariant
to the order of its two members here, as the label is, and the antisymmetric rows of
framing~B would leave it ambiguous. A pair was therefore described by the elementwise
sum and the elementwise absolute difference concatenated. The two classes are close to
balanced in the development split, at 77 cliff pairs against 68 controls, so the metric is not distorted by a rare positive. A pair is indivisible in both of the pair framings, so
its rows were assigned to the same fold and resampled together, and the pair is also
the unit of the intervals of Section~\ref{sec:eval}. Treating its two rows as
independent observations would count every pair twice and would narrow every interval
by a factor that the data does not support.

\subsection{Four descriptor sets of increasing computational
cost}\label{sec:rungs}

We compared four descriptor families, ordered by the cost of producing them, and we refer to each as a rung of that ladder. The first needs only the molecular
graph, the second a docked pose, the third an energy evaluation on that pose and the
fourth a molecular-dynamics trajectory. The ordering is the point of the comparison,
as the question is not whether a more expensive representation can be built but
whether it buys anything. We therefore scored every rung with the same algorithms, on
the same folds and in the three framings of Section~\ref{sec:framings}, so that a
difference between two rungs is a difference between representations and not between
model families. Table~\ref{tab:rungs} gives the input each rung requires, what its
descriptors capture, the feature count and the cost, with two columns that carry a
qualification. The feature counts are the two feature sets of rung~1 and, for rung~4, the full set
beside the set that survives the reliability filter. The costs are median measured values, given as processor time for the first three
rungs and as wall-clock time on one GPU for the simulation, which are not the same
unit and are not comparable beyond their order of magnitude. The number of features is
not matched across the rungs either, and that is deliberate. Each was scored in the
form in which it would actually be used, and a comparison that first cut a fingerprint
down to 34 features would describe nothing that anyone does. We also scored a
matched-dimensionality arm as a robustness check, performing the feature selection
inside each training fold so that the held-out fold can never influence which features
are chosen.

Rung~1 uses no three-dimensional information, and it is computed on the standardised
neutral structures, while every structure-based rung uses the protonated form of
Section~\ref{sec:dataset}. The ladder therefore does not describe one chemical species
throughout, so rung~1 was scored on both forms. The protonated form is never the
weaker of the two, gaining $0.018$ on \framingA{} and $0.010$ on \framingC{} and
leaving \framingB{} unchanged, so the baseline reported throughout is if anything
understated. Two feature sets were carried here, the binary ECFP4 that defined the
cliffs in Section~\ref{sec:dataset} and a count-based ECFP4 of the same width combined
with the 217 physicochemical descriptors of RDKit, and we took the better of the two
in every framing. Those 217 are the standard RDKit set, covering molecular size,
polarity, flexibility and a range of topological indices, and they enter the second
feature set alone. Neither dominates the other, and scoring only one would have
handicapped the baseline against which every other rung was measured, in the direction
that flatters the more expensive rungs.

We chose the rung-2 descriptors to test one mechanism and not to cast a wide net. That
mechanism is the proposal that two closely related inhibitors are distinguished by how
persistently each of them engages the two ends of the gorge, and by its position along
the axis between them. A fixed panel of fourteen gorge-lining residues, i.e. residues
72, 74, 86, 121, 124, 203, 286, 295, 297, 334, 337, 338, 341 and 447, carries the
pose-geometry, MM/GBSA and molecular-dynamics descriptors alike, so that all three are
computed on the same positions. Each rung-2 descriptor is the median over the three
docking seeds of Section~\ref{sec:dock}, and we kept the spread across seeds for
diagnosis but did not use it as a feature. The same descriptors were also computed
over an ensemble of receptor conformations and are reported separately as rung~2e,
because a single receptor conformation cannot accommodate every binding mode. The
ensemble is 4EY4, 4EY7, 4M0F, 6CQX, 6CQY, 6O4X, 6O5V and 6WUY, chosen by clustering on
the side-chain positions of the gorge-lining residues after superposition. Selecting
instead on resolution or on date of deposition would most likely have returned several
copies of one conformation. Each descriptor is the median across the eight, and the
five axis descriptors are dropped, being defined against the single 4EY7 geometry.
Table~\ref{tab:sirung2} names every rung-2 descriptor and marks the ones the ensemble
carries.

Rung~3 is single-trajectory, in that the complex, the protein and the ligand were
taken from the same coordinates with no re-minimisation after separation. The internal
bonded terms therefore cancel exactly and only the intermolecular interaction and the
desolvation remain, while the protein term, which is identical for every ligand under
a rigid receptor, was computed once. This is not a rigorous binding free energy and we
do not present it as one, as absolute values obtained from a single post-docking
snapshot are not defensible. It is on the ladder because it is what a structure-based
study routinely does at this cost, and it was computed on the same pose as rung~2, so
the two differ by the energy evaluation and by nothing else. Its 22 descriptors are
the interaction and desolvation terms of Table~\ref{tab:sirung3}, together with a
per-residue decomposition over the same fourteen residues. Rung~4 is described in
Section~\ref{sec:md}, and 11 of its 32 dynamic descriptors, which
Table~\ref{tab:sirung4} names one by one, survived the reliability filter described
there, which removes those whose variation between repeated simulations of one ligand
is comparable to their variation between ligands. The heavy-atom count is carried
alongside either set as a size covariate and not as a dynamic descriptor, so the two
feature counts scored are 33 and 12. We scored both sets, as reporting only one would
leave open whether a result is a property of the descriptors or of the filter. Both
the splits of Section~\ref{sec:dataset} and the designation of framing~B as the
primary comparison predate the first of these scores by more than a fortnight, so
neither was chosen once the expensive answer was known.

\subsection{Receptor model and docking protocol}\label{sec:dock}

We prepared the receptor model from the X-ray crystal structure of recombinant hAChE
in complex with donepezil, with the PDB ID being 4EY7 \citep{cheung2012} and the
resolution being \SI{2.35}{\angstrom}. Donepezil is itself a dual-binding-site
inhibitor, which is why this structure was chosen, and chain~A was retained while the
second copy in the asymmetric unit was discarded. The enzyme is shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:structure}(A), where donepezil in its crystal pose marks the active
site at the base of a narrow gorge. The architecture of that gorge is shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:structure}(B), with an aromatic residue at each end, namely Trp86 at
the catalytic anionic site (CAS) and Trp286 at the peripheral anionic site (PAS) near
the mouth. Tyr337 sits between the two, adopts different rotamers in different
complexes, and is therefore referred to as the gate, while the CZ3 atoms of the two
indole rings were measured \SI{15.6}{\angstrom} apart in this complex. Inhibitors
designed to span that distance are an established design principle and not a premise
introduced here \citep{grabowska2025}, since the peripheral anionic site has been
reported to accelerate the aggregation of $\beta$-amyloid alongside the catalytic
function of the enzyme. It is this principle that we built the descriptor sets of
Section~\ref{sec:rungs} to test. Preparation of the same receptor for simulation
differs and is described in Section~\ref{sec:md}, since it becomes relevant only once
a force field is applied, and the receptor is rigid throughout the docking.

We docked all ligands into the prepared 4EY7 receptor by using AutoDock Vina
\citep{vina_orig,vina}. The search box was a cube of \SI{30}{\angstrom} per side centred at
$(-14.257, -42.544, 30.646)$~\AA{}, midway between the CAS and the PAS so that poses
spanning the gorge were accessible, and we set the exhaustiveness to 32. Ligand structures were generated from the protonated SMILES of
Section~\ref{sec:dataset} by adding hydrogens, embedding a single conformer with the
ETKDGv3 method under a fixed random seed and optimising it with the MMFF94 force
field, all by using RDKit \citep{rdkit}. The resulting structures were converted to
the PDBQT format by using Meeko \citep{meeko}, and each ligand was docked with three
independent seeds, since the search of Vina is stochastic and a single run can settle
on a different pose. The cationic state matters here, as the catalytic site binds its
ligands through a cation--$\pi$ interaction with Trp86. The pose-geometry descriptors were computed on the top-ranked pose of each seed and
reduced to their median, and the spread across seeds was kept so that unstable poses
could be identified. Every rung above the first begins from these poses, as the MM/GBSA evaluation of
rung~3 uses the same coordinates and each simulated system is built with its ligand in
its docked pose. The docking validation therefore underwrites the whole structure-based half of the
ladder and not rung~2 alone.

\subsection{Molecular dynamics simulations and descriptor extraction}\label{sec:md}

The simulations used the same 4EY7 coordinates as the docking, prepared differently.
The crystal structure does not resolve residues 259 to 264 and 495 to 497, and the
force-field setup treated each chain as continuous and bridged both gaps with peptide
bonds of 7.11 and \SI{4.05}{\angstrom}, against an equilibrium length of
\SI{1.33}{\angstrom}. Those two bonds carried 166{,}885 of the system's
185{,}963~\si{\kilo\joule\per\mole} of bond energy, and a minimisation would have
pulled the flanking segments together and deformed the fold. Rebuilding the loops was
attempted and abandoned, as the best of four attempts gave four non-proline cis
peptide bonds in one loop of six residues against a natural rate near 0.03 per cent.
What had to go was the fabricated bond and not the absence of the loop. We therefore
divided the chain at the breaks into the segments 4--258, 265--494 and 498--541, gave
each its own chain identifier, and capped it with an acetyl group at its N-terminal
and an N-methylamide group at its C-terminal side. We used neutral caps, as charged
termini would have added six artificial formal charges on the surface, and placed them
by internal coordinates, so that the procedure is deterministic. All six caps sit on
surface loops, the nearest cap atom lying \SI{15.5}{\angstrom} from the closest
gorge-lining heavy atom and \SI{28.9}{\angstrom} from Trp86 at the gorge floor. Bond
energy fell to 1{,}145~\si{\kilo\joule\per\mole} and the maximum force from
$3.3\times10^{8}$ to $2.1\times10^{5}$~\si{\kilo\joule\per\mole\per\nano\metre}. No
atom that the two receptors share moved, the gorge-lining side chains and all 529
shared C$\alpha$ alike, their displacement being zero at the \SI{0.001}{\angstrom}
precision the coordinate file holds. The docked poses therefore remain valid and
nothing earlier in this work had to be repeated.

We built one system per ligand and not per trajectory, so that a replicate differs
from its parent only in the velocity seed and tests the sampling and not the
structure. The protein was described by the amber14SB force field \citep{amber_ff14sb}, the
ligand by OpenFF Sage 2.3.0 \citep{openff_sage} through a SMIRNOFF template generator
and the solvent by the TIP3P water model \citep{tip3p} with sodium chloride at
\SI{0.15}{\mole\per\litre}. This is the
combination we used for the MM/GBSA calculation of Section~\ref{sec:rungs}, as a
difference there would have turned a comparison of rungs into a comparison of force
fields. Each ligand was placed in its docked pose in a cubic box of \SI{100.1}{\angstrom} per
side, giving between 96{,}220 and 96{,}298 atoms. Table~\ref{tab:system} reports the
composition, where a range is the minimum and the maximum across systems and a single
value means every system shares it. Two quantities were checked for every system instead of being assumed, the first being
the net charge after counterions, which is at most $2.8\times10^{-13}\,e$ across all
217 systems. The second is the shortest receptor--ligand heavy-atom distance at build
time, which runs from 1.67 to \SI{2.64}{\angstrom}. The lower end of that range is a tight contact, which we state openly, and what
resolves it is the minimisation and equilibration that follow.

Each system was energy-minimised by steepest descent until the maximum force fell
below \SI{100}{\kilo\joule\per\mole\per\nano\metre}. It was then equilibrated for
\SI{100}{\pico\second} in the canonical ensemble with the solute position-restrained
and for a further \SI{100}{\pico\second} in the isothermal-isobaric ensemble without
restraints. Production used a \SI{2}{\femto\second} time step with the bonds to
hydrogen constrained by LINCS, a Verlet neighbour list and particle-mesh Ewald
electrostatics. The cutoff was \SI{1.0}{\nano\metre} for both the real-space Coulomb
and the van der Waals interactions, with a long-range dispersion correction applied to
the energy and the pressure. Temperature was held by the velocity-rescaling thermostat
at \SI{300}{\kelvin} with a coupling time of \SI{0.1}{\pico\second}, and pressure by
the c-rescale barostat at \SI{1}{\bar}, isotropic, with a coupling time of
\SI{2}{\pico\second} and a compressibility of $4.5\times10^{-5}$~\si{\per\bar}, with
coordinates written to disk every \SI{10}{\pico\second}. We ran the simulations with the GPU-resident mdrun of GROMACS \citep{gromacs}, which
keeps the whole step on the accelerator. We
completed 229 production runs over the 217 ligands, 211 of them simulated once and 6
three times for the replicate pilot, amounting to 6{,}965~ns. Production was
\SI{25}{\nano\second} per ligand, of which the first \SI{5}{\nano\second} was
discarded and the \SI{20}{\nano\second} that follows supplied every descriptor, so
every ligand carries the same record. Fifty-six runs of the first batch were continued
to \SI{50}{\nano\second}, and they enter this work only as the evidence that
\SI{20}{\nano\second} of analysed trajectory is not too short.

Thirty-two descriptors were computed from each trajectory, and they fall into the
three design classes of Figure~\ref{fig:mdflow}. Persistence quantities measure the
fraction of the analysed window over which a contact or a position is held,
fluctuation quantities the amplitude of motion, and event counts the rate at which
something happens. None of them is a frame-averaged version of a rung-2 quantity, since
averaging a pose descriptor over a trajectory would test the averaging rather than the
dynamics. The fluctuation class rests on the root-mean-square fluctuation of an atom
about its own mean position, which is the quantity a trajectory supplies and a single
structure cannot \citep{mdfluct,rmsf_bfactor}, taken over the ligand atoms and over the
gorge-lining side chains. Two event counts follow the Tyr337 side chain, whose rotation
gates the gorge \citep{gating1998,gorge2017} and which occupies the position of Phe330
in the Torpedo enzyme where that gating was characterised. One further quantity counts
the water molecules hydrogen bonding to ligand and protein at the same time, since a
bridging water participates in recognition rather than being displaced by it
\citep{water_ladbury,water_levy}. They are computed on the same fourteen gorge-lining
residues, with Trp86 at the CAS, Trp286 at the PAS and Tyr337 as the gate, and the
heavy-atom count is carried beside them as a size covariate rather than as one of
the 32. Extracting them requires
the periodic boundary to be handled before anything is measured. Each molecule is made
whole along its bonds, prevented from jumping between consecutive frames and placed in
the periodic image of the first frame, and only then is the receptor superposed by the
Kabsch algorithm, all of it through MDAnalysis \citep{mdanalysis}. An earlier version did none of this and compared coordinates against
a frozen reference, which absorbed the rotation and the diffusion of the complex and
left molecules cut by box faces. Re-extracting one raw trajectory under both
treatments changed 19 of some 30 descriptors, with the ligand RMSD falling from 26.70
to \SI{3.14}{\angstrom} and the gorge fluctuation from 37.09 to \SI{0.93}{\angstrom}.
The contact occupancies moved as well, and every one of them upward, since a contact
whose partner was written into another periodic image is a contact that was missed.

Which of the 32 descriptors can carry a pair endpoint was decided by a replicate
pilot, and the rule was fixed before any replicate had been scored. A descriptor
qualifies only when the spread between ligands is at least twice the spread between
replicates of the same ligand, which is the reliability
$\sigma^2_{\mathrm{lig}}/(\sigma^2_{\mathrm{lig}} + 2\sigma^2_{\mathrm{rep}})$ written
as a ratio of standard deviations. The factor of two is a judgement, but it was made
blind, and the pair endpoint justifies it, because a difference between two ligands
carries the replicate noise twice over. The replicate spread rests on six ligands simulated three times each, which is a
narrow basis and is stated here as such, while the spread between ligands is taken
over all 217. Of the 160 combinations of descriptor and analysis window that were
tested, 100 are dominated by the velocity seed rather than by the ligand. At the
\SI{20}{\nano\second} window 11 of the 32 descriptors pass, and 9 of those are contact
occupancies, so of the three design classes only persistence survives in quantity. A duration ablation could not
have answered this question, since a quantity can be flat within every individual run
and still differ between seeds. The Tyr337 gate flips about once in three
trajectories, so a run that begins in one rotamer reports that rotamer with a flat
RMSD and no drift, and looks converged while being stuck.

\subsection{Preprocessing and learning algorithms}\label{sec:algo}

Every preprocessing step was a stage of a pipeline rather than a transformation applied
to the matrix beforehand, so each was fitted on the training part of a fold and then
applied unchanged to the held-out part. The structure-based rungs began with a median
imputation, since a pose or a trajectory occasionally failed to supply what a
descriptor needs. We took that median from the training fold alone, as a replacement
computed on the whole matrix would carry information from the data it is then asked to
predict. A zero-variance filter followed, removing the fingerprint bits and the
per-residue counts that were constant across the ligands of a fold. We then
standardised the features for the linear, latent-variable, kernel and distance-based
learners, whose behaviour depends on the relative scale of the features, and left the
two tree ensembles unscaled, since they are invariant to any monotone rescaling.
Rung~1 needed no imputation stage, as the non-finite values that RDKit returns for a
handful of its topological descriptors were set to zero when the matrix was built.

Six regression families carried framings~A and~B, chosen to span the model classes
rather than to find the best one. They were ridge regression \citep{ridge}, partial
least squares \citep{pls}, $k$-nearest neighbours \citep{knn}, a support vector machine
with a radial basis function kernel \citep{svm}, a random forest \citep{rf} and
histogram-based gradient boosting \citep{gbm}, all as implemented in scikit-learn
\citep{sklearn}. A linear model, a latent-variable model, an instance-based one, a
kernel method and two ensembles between them cover the shapes a structure-activity
relationship is usually assumed to take, so a rung that fails on all six has not simply
been given the wrong model. We fixed the hyperparameters before any rung was scored and
used the same values at every rung, with a ridge penalty of 10, five latent components,
five neighbours, a kernel penalty of 10 and 500 trees, the remaining values being the
library defaults. We ran no inner search over them, because tuning each rung separately
would let a comparison between representations absorb a difference in how hard each one
had been tuned.

Framing~C carried five classification families, namely logistic regression, the same
$k$-nearest neighbours, the same support vector machine with probability estimates
enabled, a random forest and histogram-based gradient boosting. The suite is one
smaller than the regression suite because partial least squares has no classification
counterpart, so it is missing there rather than having been tried and failed. Every
rung met the same families in the same framings, which is what allows a difference
between two rungs to be read as a difference between their descriptors. A family
appearing in both suites kept its hyperparameters, so the regression and the
classification results differ by the task and by the metric and not by the
configuration of the learner. Section~\ref{sec:eval} reports the best value a rung
reached over its descriptor arms and its families, and
Tables~\ref{tab:benchreg} and~\ref{tab:benchclf} hold the full grid behind it.

We scored a matched-dimensionality arm alongside the main comparison, in which the
wider feature set is cut to the width of the narrower one before the learner sees it.
The selector is a univariate filter on the $F$ statistic, of the regression or the
classification form according to the framing, and we placed it inside the pipeline with
everything else. That placement is the point of the arm rather than a detail of its
implementation, as choosing the $k$ features on the whole matrix and cross-validating
afterwards lets the held-out fold influence which features it is then asked to predict
from. The inflation that follows is substantial and it is also invisible, since such an
arm still reports a cross-validated score that looks honest. Holding the selection
inside the fold cost nothing beyond the time to refit it five times.

\subsection{Model evaluation and statistical analysis}\label{sec:eval}

Every model was scored by five-fold cross-validation on the folds frozen in
Section~\ref{sec:dataset}, and we pooled the out-of-fold predictions before computing
any metric, so that a fold which happens to be easy cannot offset one which is hard.
The number reported for a rung is the best it reached over its descriptor arms and its
algorithm families, and the same selection is made on both sides of every comparison.
The two regression framings were judged primarily by the coefficient of determination,
with the root mean squared error, the mean absolute error and the Spearman rank
correlation recorded beside it. The rank correlation is kept because a descriptor set
can order the differences within pairs correctly while misjudging their size, and the
two metrics together separate that case from a set which does neither. We also
recorded the observed and predicted means over the cliff pairs and the control pairs
separately, since a model can otherwise reach a respectable coefficient of
determination on the pooled pairs by predicting the controls well and the cliffs not at
all. Framing~C was judged by the area under the receiver operating characteristic
curve, which suits a design of 77 cliff pairs against 68 controls. Average precision,
the Matthews correlation coefficient and balanced accuracy were recorded alongside it,
the last two at a probability of one half. The sealed test split was never opened, so
every number in this work is a development-split estimate.

Comparisons of a descriptor between cliff pairs and control pairs were made with the
two-sided Mann--Whitney $U$ test, since those differences are bounded below by zero and
strongly skewed. Monotone association was measured by the Spearman rank correlation,
and the Pearson correlation appears only where a linear relation is itself the claim.
Any comparison of this kind is confounded by molecular size, as the two members of a
cliff pair differ by 4.4 heavy atoms on average against 1.8 for a control pair. A
descriptor that tracks size then separates the groups for a reason unrelated to
potency. We
therefore repeated each test within bins of equal heavy-atom difference and again on
the residual after regressing the descriptor on that difference, and treated a
descriptor as surviving only where it held in both. These tests are descriptive and
carry no correction for multiplicity, as the inferential weight of this work rests on
the primary endpoint fixed in advance and on the interval around it.

An interval on each comparison came from a bootstrap over the units of its framing,
with 300 replicates. The unit is the ligand in framing~A and the pair in framings~B
and~C, and a pair was resampled whole, so its two antisymmetric rows travel together.
Inside every replicate we re-selected the best over the descriptor arms and the
algorithm families on both sides, because choosing the best is part of the procedure
whose uncertainty is being measured. Rung~1 entered with both of its feature sets for
the same reason, as the winner between them depends on the framing. We took the
interval as the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the resampled gap. It carries a bias
we state rather than correct, since running folds fixed beforehand over a resample
puts duplicated units on both sides of a fold boundary and inflates the absolute
scores. That inflation applies identically to the two sides, so the gap is far less
affected than either level, and a bootstrap median is accordingly not a performance
estimate and must not be quoted as one. The resolution of the comparison was measured
rather than assumed, by pushing synthetic descriptor matrices through the same support
vector machine and the same folds, with the rung-1 baseline re-simulated in every
replicate so that its own uncertainty is carried. A gain of 0.26 in the coefficient of
determination is detected in 90 per cent of simulations and a gain of 0.11 in 36 per
cent, with a mean interval width of 0.32, which is the resolution this design can
claim.
