\section{Methods}

In this section, we introduce the procedures to construct and to evaluate the
descriptor sets compared in this work. We first describe the target and its binding
site, the docking protocol and its validation, and the construction of the
activity-cliff dataset. We then describe the four descriptor sets, the molecular
dynamics simulations from which the last of them is derived, and the model suite on
which all of them are scored.

\subsection{Target and binding site}

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Figure1_hAChE_structure.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Human acetylcholinesterase and its catalytic gorge (PDB 4EY7).}
    (A)~Overall fold, rainbow-coloured from N- to C-terminus; donepezil in the
    crystal pose marks the gorge as black spheres. (B)~The architecture of the gorge, formed by Trp86 at
    the catalytic anionic site, Trp286 at the peripheral anionic site, the swinging
    Tyr337 gate between them, and the Ser203--His447--Glu334 catalytic triad.
    The Trp86--Trp286 CZ3--CZ3 separation is 15.6~\AA{}, the span a bis-ligand must
    bridge.}
  \label{fig:structure}
\end{figure}

Human acetylcholinesterase (hAChE) is a therapeutic target in Alzheimer's disease.
The enzyme hydrolyses acetylcholine, and its inhibition raises the synaptic
concentration of this neurotransmitter, which is the basis of the cholinergic
treatment of the disease. Beyond this catalytic function, the peripheral anionic
site of the enzyme has been reported to accelerate the aggregation of
$\beta$-amyloid, and inhibitors that span both the catalytic and the peripheral site
are therefore designed to address the two functions simultaneously
\citep{grabowska2025}. Donepezil, which is the ligand in the structure used in this
work, is such a dual-binding-site inhibitor. The simultaneous engagement of the two
ends of the gorge is thus an established design principle rather than a premise
introduced here, and it is this principle that the descriptor sets described in
the present work are constructed to test.

The receptor model is prepared from the X-ray crystal structure of recombinant hAChE
in complex with donepezil, with the PDB ID being 4EY7 \citep{cheung2012}, which is
solved at a resolution of \SI{2.35}{\angstrom}. Chain~A is retained and the second
copy in the asymmetric unit is discarded. The active site is located at the base of a
narrow gorge, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:structure}, and the gorge consists of two
aromatic residues at its two ends, a gate residue between them, and the catalytic
triad. Trp86 is located at the catalytic anionic site (CAS) adjacent to the
Ser203--His447--Glu334 triad, while Trp286 is located at the peripheral anionic site
(PAS) near the mouth of the gorge. Tyr337 is located between the two sites and adopts
different rotamers in different complexes, and is therefore referred to as the gate.
In the 4EY7 complex, the distance between the CZ3 atoms of the Trp86 and Trp286
indole rings is measured as \SI{15.6}{\angstrom}, and donepezil is observed to span
this distance and to contact both residues.

The design principle described above is translated into a testable form in terms of
this architecture, namely that two closely related inhibitors are distinguished by
how persistently each of them engages the two ends of the gorge and by its position
along the axis between them. A fixed panel of fourteen gorge-lining residues, i.e.
residues 72, 74, 86, 121, 124, 203, 286, 295, 297, 334, 337, 338, 341 and 447, is
used throughout this work, so that the pose-geometry, MM/GBSA and molecular-dynamics
descriptors are calculated on the same positions and remain comparable with one
another.
The molecular-dynamics descriptors defined in Section~\ref{sec:mddesc} follow
directly from this geometry, i.e. the contact occupancies at Trp86 and at Trp286 and
at both residues simultaneously, the rotameric behaviour of Tyr337, and the
projection of the ligand on the Trp86--Trp286 axis. The preparation of the receptor,
including the treatment of the loops that are not resolved in the crystal structure,
is described in Section~\ref{sec:md}, since it becomes relevant only once a force
field is applied.

\subsection{Docking protocol and its validation}

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Figure2_redocking_validation.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Redocking validation, and the limit of a rigid receptor.}
    (A)~Donepezil redocked into its own structure reproduces the crystal pose to
    0.75~\AA{} RMSD over five seeds. (B)~Huperzine~A docked into its own receptor
    (4EY5) succeeds. (C)~The same ligand cross-docked into 4EY7 fails at
    3.10~\AA{}, because the Tyr337 rotamer of this structure occludes the true
    pose.}
  \label{fig:redock}
\end{figure}

All ligands are docked into the prepared 4EY7 receptor by using AutoDock Vina
\citep{vina} through its Python bindings. The search box is a cube of
\SI{30}{\angstrom} per side, centred at $(-14.257, -42.544, 30.646)$~\AA{}, which
lies midway between the CAS and the PAS so that poses spanning the gorge are
accessible. The exhaustiveness is set to 32. Ligand structures are generated from the
standardised SMILES by adding hydrogens, embedding a single conformer with the
ETKDGv3 method under a fixed random seed, and optimising it with the MMFF94 force
field, all of which is performed by using RDKit \citep{rdkit}; the resulting
structures are converted to the PDBQT format by using Meeko \citep{meeko}. Each
ligand is docked with three independent seeds, and the pose-geometry descriptors
defined in Section~\ref{sec:rungs} are calculated on the top-ranked pose of each seed
and reduced to their median, with the spread across seeds retained so that unstable
poses can be identified.

The protocol is validated by redocking four ligands whose complexes with hAChE have
been solved, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:redock}, with five seeds per ligand and a
pose counted as successful when its RMSD from the crystal pose is below
\SI{2.0}{\angstrom}. Donepezil, which is the ligand of 4EY7 itself, is reproduced at
$0.745 \pm 0.009$~\AA{} in five of five runs, and territrem~B is reproduced at
$0.740 \pm 0.012$~\AA{} in five of five runs. The remaining two ligands are not
reproduced as well. Galantamine gives $2.463 \pm 1.709$~\AA{} and succeeds in four of
five runs, and huperzine~A gives $3.098 \pm 0.011$~\AA{} and fails in all five. The
failure of huperzine~A is systematic rather than stochastic, since the spread across
seeds is only \SI{0.011}{\angstrom}, and it therefore requires an explanation before
the protocol can be used.

Two explanations are tested. The first is that the search box is too large for small
ligands, which is examined by repeating the docking with boxes of 18, 22, 26 and
\SI{30}{\angstrom} per side. The RMSD of huperzine~A is between 3.05 and
\SI{3.18}{\angstrom} across all four box sizes, so box size is not the cause. The
second is that the single receptor conformation of 4EY7 cannot accommodate these
ligands. This is examined by docking each ligand into the structure of its own
complex, and both of them succeed, since huperzine~A is reproduced at
\SI{0.23}{\angstrom} in 4EY5 and galantamine at \SI{0.81}{\angstrom} in 4EY6. The side chain of Tyr337, which
forms the gate between the two sites, differs by \SI{2.53}{\angstrom} between 4EY5
and 4EY7 and occludes the true huperzine~A pose in the latter. The limitation is
therefore the rigidity of the receptor rather than the docking protocol, which is the
observation that motivates the ensemble-docking procedure described in
Section~\ref{sec:rungs} and, ultimately, the molecular dynamics of
Section~\ref{sec:md}. A third observation from the same experiments is that Vina is
strongly stochastic for small ligands, since galantamine gave 5.88 and
\SI{1.60}{\angstrom} on different seeds under otherwise identical settings, and this
is the reason that seeds are fixed and three replicates are run for every ligand.

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_interactions.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{The reference binding mode, and docking checked at the level of
    contacts.} (a)~Donepezil in the crystal pose with the residues it contacts,
    where the colour of each edge gives the type of interaction. (b)~The same
    fingerprint calculated for the redocked pose.}
  \label{fig:interactions}
\end{figure}

Since the pose-geometry and molecular-dynamics descriptors are defined in terms of
residue contacts
rather than RMSD, the protocol is validated a second time at the level of
interactions, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:interactions}. Interaction fingerprints are
calculated for the crystal pose and for the redocked pose of donepezil by using
ProLIF \citep{prolif2021}, with bond orders transferred from the docked structure so
that the two poses carry identical chemistry and differ only in their coordinates.
Six residues are contacted in both poses, five in the crystal pose only, and two in
the docked pose only, and the colour of each edge in the figure gives the type of
interaction that is detected. Every typed interaction at the three named sites is
recovered, i.e. the hydrophobic and $\pi$-stacking interactions at Trp86 and at
Trp286 and the cation--$\pi$ and hydrophobic interactions at Tyr337, and what is lost
on docking is van der Waals contact alone with Ser203 and His447. The binding mode on which the
later descriptors depend is therefore reproduced, although the typing itself is used
only for validation here and is not carried into any descriptor set.

\paragraph{At target screening: stereochemical cliffs.} Enantiomer and
diastereomer pairs reach Tanimoto 1.0 under ECFP, which does not encode
stereochemistry, and a naive similarity-plus-potency criterion counts them as
maximal-similarity cliffs. No stereo-blind fingerprint can resolve such a pair by
construction while any 3D method resolves it trivially, so a benchmark dominated by
them is biased toward structure before a model is fitted. This is what disqualified
four of the six targets screened: BACE1 returned 101 stereochemical cliffs against
5 constitutional. Acetylcholinesterase inverts that, 100 against 10, and
\textbf{every pair in the final set is constitutional} -- \texttt{same\_formula} is
false for all 97 and the minimum heavy-atom difference is 1.

\paragraph{At pair selection: binary saturation.} A separate problem affects the
pairs that do enter. Binary ECFP4 reports Tanimoto exactly 1.000 for 49 of the 97
constitutional cliff pairs, molecules a median of four and up to eight heavy atoms
apart; count-based ECFP4 separates the same pairs at a median of 0.878. Folding
counts to bits discards the multiplicity that distinguishes them.
