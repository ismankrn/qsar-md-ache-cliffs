\subsection{Two fingerprint artefacts, at two different stages}
\TODO{Keep these separate -- an earlier draft of this subsection merged them and
  attributed the wrong one to the AChE set.}

\paragraph{At target screening: stereochemical cliffs.} Enantiomer and
diastereomer pairs reach Tanimoto 1.0 under ECFP, which does not encode
stereochemistry, and a naive similarity-plus-potency criterion counts them as
maximal-similarity cliffs. No stereo-blind fingerprint can resolve such a pair by
construction while any 3D method resolves it trivially, so a benchmark dominated by
them is biased toward structure before a model is fitted. This is what disqualified
four of the six targets screened: BACE1 returned 101 stereochemical cliffs against
5 constitutional. Acetylcholinesterase inverts that, 100 against 10, and
\textbf{every pair in the final set is constitutional} -- \texttt{same\_formula} is
false for all 97 and the minimum heavy-atom difference is 1.

\paragraph{At pair selection: binary saturation.} A separate problem affects the
pairs that do enter. Binary ECFP4 reports Tanimoto exactly 1.000 for 49 of the 97
constitutional cliff pairs, molecules a median of four and up to eight heavy atoms
apart; count-based ECFP4 separates the same pairs at a median of 0.878. Folding
counts to bits discards the multiplicity that distinguishes them.
