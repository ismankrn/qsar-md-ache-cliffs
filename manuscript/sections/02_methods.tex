\section{Methods}

In this section, we introduce the procedures to construct and to evaluate the
descriptor sets compared in this work. We first describe the target and its binding
site, the docking protocol and its validation, and the construction of the
activity-cliff dataset. We then describe the four descriptor sets, the molecular
dynamics simulations from which the last of them was derived, and the model suite on
which all of them were scored.

\subsection{Target and binding site}\label{sec:site}

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Figure1_hAChE_structure.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Human acetylcholinesterase and its catalytic gorge (PDB 4EY7).}
    (A)~Overall fold, rainbow-coloured from N- to C-terminus; donepezil in the
    crystal pose marks the gorge as black spheres. (B)~The architecture of the gorge, formed by Trp86 at
    the catalytic anionic site, Trp286 at the peripheral anionic site, the swinging
    Tyr337 gate between them, and the Ser203--His447--Glu334 catalytic triad.
    The Trp86--Trp286 CZ3--CZ3 separation is 15.6~\AA{}, the span a bis-ligand must
    bridge.}
  \label{fig:structure}
\end{figure}

Human acetylcholinesterase (hAChE) is a therapeutic target in Alzheimer's disease.
The enzyme hydrolyses acetylcholine, and its inhibition raises the synaptic
concentration of this neurotransmitter, which is the basis of the cholinergic
treatment of the disease. Beyond this catalytic function, the peripheral anionic
site of the enzyme has been reported to accelerate the aggregation of
$\beta$-amyloid, and inhibitors that span both the catalytic and the peripheral site
are therefore designed to address the two functions simultaneously
\citep{grabowska2025}. Donepezil, which was the ligand in the structure used in this
work, is such a dual-binding-site inhibitor. The simultaneous engagement of the two
ends of the gorge is thus an established design principle rather than a premise
introduced here, and it was this principle that the descriptor sets described in the
present work were constructed to test.

The receptor model was prepared from the X-ray crystal structure of recombinant hAChE
in complex with donepezil, with the PDB ID being 4EY7 \citep{cheung2012} and the
resolution being \SI{2.35}{\angstrom}. Chain~A was retained and the second copy in
the asymmetric unit was discarded. The overall fold is shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:structure}(A), in which the position of the gorge is marked by
donepezil in its crystal pose. The active site is located at the base of this narrow
gorge, and its architecture is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:structure}(B), where the
gorge consists of two aromatic residues at its two ends, a gate residue between them,
and the catalytic triad. Trp86 is located at the catalytic anionic site (CAS)
adjacent to the Ser203--His447--Glu334 triad, while Trp286 is located at the
peripheral anionic site (PAS) near the mouth of the gorge. Tyr337 is located between
the two sites and adopts different rotamers in different complexes, and is therefore
referred to as the gate. In the 4EY7 complex, the distance between the CZ3 atoms of
the Trp86 and Trp286 indole rings was measured as \SI{15.6}{\angstrom}, and donepezil
was observed to span this distance and to contact both residues.

The design principle described above was translated into a testable form in terms of
this architecture, namely that two closely related inhibitors are distinguished by
how persistently each of them engages the two ends of the gorge and by its position
along the axis between them. A fixed panel of fourteen gorge-lining residues, i.e.
residues 72, 74, 86, 121, 124, 203, 286, 295, 297, 334, 337, 338, 341 and 447, was
used throughout this work, so that the pose-geometry, MM/GBSA and molecular-dynamics
descriptors were calculated on the same positions and remained comparable with one
another. The molecular-dynamics descriptors defined in Section~\ref{sec:mddesc}
followed directly from this geometry, i.e. the contact occupancies at Trp86 and at
Trp286 and at both residues simultaneously, the rotameric behaviour of Tyr337, and
the projection of the ligand on the Trp86--Trp286 axis. The preparation of the
receptor, including the treatment of the loops that are not resolved in the crystal
structure, is described in Section~\ref{sec:md}, since it became relevant only once a
force field was applied.

\subsection{Docking protocol and its validation}\label{sec:dock}

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Figure2_redocking_validation.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Redocking validation, and the limit of a rigid receptor.}
    (A)~Donepezil redocked into its own structure reproduces the crystal pose to
    0.75~\AA{} RMSD over five seeds. (B)~Huperzine~A docked into its own receptor
    (4EY5) succeeds. (C)~The same ligand cross-docked into 4EY7 fails at
    3.10~\AA{}, because the Tyr337 rotamer of this structure occludes the true
    pose.}
  \label{fig:redock}
\end{figure}

All ligands were docked into the prepared 4EY7 receptor by using AutoDock Vina
\citep{vina} through its Python bindings. The search box was a cube of
\SI{30}{\angstrom} per side, centred at $(-14.257, -42.544, 30.646)$~\AA{}, which
lies midway between the CAS and the PAS so that poses spanning the gorge were
accessible, and the exhaustiveness was set to 32. Ligand structures were generated
from the standardised SMILES by adding hydrogens, by embedding a single conformer
with the ETKDGv3 method under a fixed random seed, and by optimising it with the
MMFF94 force field, all of which was performed by using RDKit \citep{rdkit}, and the
resulting structures were converted to the PDBQT format by using Meeko
\citep{meeko}. Each ligand was docked with three independent seeds, and the
pose-geometry descriptors defined in Section~\ref{sec:rungs} were calculated on the
top-ranked pose of each seed and reduced to their median, with the spread across
seeds retained so that unstable poses could be identified.

The protocol was validated by redocking four ligands whose complexes with hAChE have
been solved, with five seeds per ligand and a pose counted as successful when its
RMSD from the crystal pose was below \SI{2.0}{\angstrom}. Donepezil, which was the
ligand of 4EY7 itself, was reproduced at $0.745 \pm 0.009$~\AA{} in five of five
runs, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:redock}(A), and territrem~B was reproduced at
$0.740 \pm 0.012$~\AA{} in five of five runs. The remaining two ligands were not
reproduced as well. Galantamine gave $2.463 \pm 1.709$~\AA{} and succeeded in four of
five runs, and huperzine~A gave $3.098 \pm 0.011$~\AA{} and failed in all five. The
failure of huperzine~A was systematic rather than stochastic, since the spread across
seeds was only \SI{0.011}{\angstrom}, and it therefore required an explanation before
the protocol could be used.

Two explanations were tested. The first was that the search box is too large for
small ligands, which was examined by repeating the docking with boxes of 18, 22, 26
and \SI{30}{\angstrom} per side. The RMSD of huperzine~A was between 3.05 and
\SI{3.18}{\angstrom} across all four box sizes, so that box size was not the cause.
The second was that the single receptor conformation of 4EY7 cannot accommodate these
ligands. This was examined by docking each ligand into the structure of its own
complex, and both of them succeeded, since huperzine~A was reproduced at
\SI{0.23}{\angstrom} in 4EY5, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:redock}(B), and galantamine
at \SI{0.81}{\angstrom} in 4EY6. The same ligand and the same protocol failed in
4EY7, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:redock}(C), so that the two panels differ only in
the receptor. The side chain of Tyr337, which forms the gate between the two sites,
differs by \SI{2.53}{\angstrom} between 4EY5 and 4EY7 and occludes the true
huperzine~A pose in the latter. The limitation was therefore the rigidity of the
receptor rather than the docking protocol, which is the observation that motivated
the ensemble-docking procedure described in Section~\ref{sec:rungs} and, ultimately,
the molecular dynamics of Section~\ref{sec:md}. A third observation from the same
experiments was that Vina is strongly stochastic for small ligands, since galantamine
gave 5.88 and \SI{1.60}{\angstrom} on different seeds under otherwise identical
settings, and this was the reason that seeds were fixed and that three replicates
were run for every ligand.

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_interactions.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{The reference binding mode, and docking checked at the level of
    contacts.} (a)~Donepezil in the crystal pose with the residues it contacts,
    where the colour of each edge gives the type of interaction. (b)~The same
    fingerprint calculated for the redocked pose.}
  \label{fig:interactions}
\end{figure}

Since the pose-geometry and molecular-dynamics descriptors are defined in terms of
residue contacts rather than RMSD, the protocol was validated a second time at the
level of interactions. Interaction fingerprints were calculated for the crystal pose
and for the redocked pose of donepezil by using ProLIF \citep{prolif2021}, with bond
orders transferred from the docked structure so that the two poses carried identical
chemistry and differed only in their coordinates. The binding mode of the crystal
pose is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:interactions}(a), in which every residue contacting
the ligand is drawn and the colour of the connecting edge gives the type of
interaction that was detected. The two fingerprints are compared in
Figure~\ref{fig:interactions}(b), which resolves each contact by residue and by
interaction type and distinguishes the crystal pose from the docked one. Six residues
were contacted in both poses, five in the crystal pose only, and two in the docked
pose only. Every typed interaction at the three named sites was recovered, i.e. the
hydrophobic and $\pi$-stacking interactions at Trp86 and at Trp286 and the
cation--$\pi$ and hydrophobic interactions at Tyr337, and what was lost on docking was
van der Waals contact alone with Ser203 and His447. The binding mode on which the
later descriptors depend was therefore reproduced, although the typing itself was
used only for validation here and was not carried into any descriptor set.

\subsection{Dataset and activity-cliff definition}\label{sec:dataset}

The dataset was constructed in three steps, i.e. the selection of the target, the
curation of its activity data, and the assembly of pairs of near-identical ligands
that differ sharply in potency together with pairs that are equally similar but do
not. Pairs of the first kind were further divided according to whether their two
members differ in constitution or only in stereochemistry, which was decided by
comparing the canonical SMILES of the two members with the stereochemistry stripped,
and only the constitutional pairs were treated as informative. The reason is that
ECFP does not encode stereochemistry, so that no stereo-blind fingerprint can resolve
a stereoisomer pair by construction while any method that uses three-dimensional
coordinates resolves it trivially, and a set dominated by such pairs would favour
structure-based descriptors before a single model had been fitted. Six candidate
targets were then screened against three entry criteria, namely at least 80 compounds
surviving curation, a potency range of at least 3 log units, and at least 25
constitutional activity cliffs. All six satisfied the first two criteria and two of
them satisfied the third, and acetylcholinesterase was selected from those two
because it was the target in which the constitutional pairs dominated most strongly
and because the dual-site architecture described in Section~\ref{sec:site} gives a
mechanistic reason to expect its cliffs to be geometric in origin. The counts behind
this choice are reported in Section~\ref{sec:composition}.

Activity data for CHEMBL220 were retrieved from ChEMBL, and a record was retained
only when it came from a binding assay, reported IC\textsubscript{50},
$K_\mathrm{i}$ or $K_\mathrm{d}$ with an exact relation and in nanomolar units,
carried a pChEMBL value, was free of the data-validity flags assigned by ChEMBL
itself, and was not marked as a potential duplicate. A single assay type was then
retained, which was the most frequent one and was IC\textsubscript{50} in this case,
since a pair formed from an IC\textsubscript{50} measurement and a $K_\mathrm{i}$
measurement can satisfy a cliff criterion by virtue of the assay rather than of the
chemistry. Structures were standardised by stripping salts, by retaining the largest
covalent fragment as the parent, and by neutralising formal charges introduced by the
deposited representation, and structures below six heavy atoms were removed.
Replicate measurements of the same standardised structure were collapsed to their
median, and a structure whose replicates disagreed by more than one log unit,
measured as their standard deviation, was discarded rather than averaged, because a
compound measured inconsistently can enter a cliff pair on the strength of its own
measurement error. The curated set consisted of 5{,}609 compounds spanning pChEMBL
4.00 to 10.96.

Structural similarity was computed as the Tanimoto coefficient on binary ECFP4
fingerprints, i.e. Morgan fingerprints of radius 2 folded to 2{,}048 bits. A pair of
compounds was treated as near-identical when its Tanimoto coefficient was at least
0.90, and as an activity cliff when, in addition, the two members differed in pChEMBL
by at least 2.0 log units, which corresponds to a hundredfold difference in potency.
Within the curated set, 421 pairs cleared the similarity criterion with a potency
difference of at least one log unit, 110 of these reached the hundredfold criterion,
and 100 of those 110 were constitutional. The compound pool was then filtered for
simulation feasibility, with a compound retained only when it was composed of the
elements H, C, N, O, F, S, Cl, Br, I and P, carried no radical electrons, had a
molecular weight between 150 and 700~Da, had at most 55 heavy atoms and at most 18
rotatable bonds, and could be embedded in three dimensions by ETKDGv3. This filter
removed 239 of the 5{,}609 compounds, most of them on molecular weight or on
rotatable-bond count. It was applied to the whole pool before any pairing and not to
the pairs afterwards, because a pair is lost as soon as either of its two members is
removed, so that filtering the pool first kept every surviving pair intact by
construction. Of the 100 constitutional cliff pairs, 97 survived this filter with both
members intact, and those 97 pairs were formed from 99 distinct compounds.

A control pair was defined as a pair that was near-identical under the same
similarity floor of 0.90 but whose two members differed in pChEMBL by at most 0.30
log units, i.e. by less than twofold, so that the two groups differed in the property
under test and in as little else as possible. Stereoisomer pairs were excluded here
on the same grounds as above, and controls were drawn so as to reproduce the joint
distribution of similarity and mean potency of the cliff pairs, binned into four
similarity intervals and five potency intervals, with each control compound used in
one pair only. The procedure yielded 86 control pairs formed from 172 compounds, and
the matching was close on both matched quantities, since the median Tanimoto
coefficient was 1.000 for the cliff pairs against 0.993 for the controls and the mean
of the pair-mean potencies was 6.64 against 6.58, while the quantity under test
differed by construction, with a mean potency difference of 2.66 log units against
0.13. The final set therefore consisted of 271 ligands and 183 pairs, i.e. 97 cliff
pairs and 86 control pairs. A ligand was prepared and simulated once while a pair
refers to two ligands, since a compound may belong to more than one pair, and the two
tables were kept separate throughout for this reason. The dominant protonation state
of each ligand at pH 7.4 was assigned by a set of explicit rules, and every ligand in
the final set carried a net positive charge, with 170 ligands at $+1$ and 101 at
$+2$, which is consistent with the cation--$\pi$ recognition at Trp86 described in
Section~\ref{sec:site}. The division of this set into a development part and a sealed
test part is described in Section~\ref{sec:splits}.

\subsection{Splits}\label{sec:splits}

The development and the test split were fixed once, in a separate step that wrote them
to a file, and every descriptor set described below was scored on exactly those
splits. Recomputing the folds inside each model would have let the comparison measure
the luck of the fold as much as the quality of the descriptors, since a difference
between two descriptor sets scored on two different partitions of the same data is not
attributable to either of them. Fixing the splits before any molecular-dynamics result
had been produced also removed the possibility of adjusting them, knowingly or
otherwise, in a direction that flatters a conclusion. The random seed was 20260902 and
was recorded together with the split assignment.

Splitting at the level of the ligand would have been incorrect here, because the
ligands are not independent units. Each ligand belongs to at least one pair, and
placing one member of a cliff pair in the training set and the other in the test set
would allow a model to memorise the shared scaffold and to recover the difference
within the pair without having represented anything about the cliff. The splits were
therefore built from the connected components of the graph whose vertices are the
ligands and whose edges are the pairs, so that a component is the smallest unit that
can be moved from one split to another without separating the two members of a pair.
The 271 ligands formed 110 such components, of between 2 and 9 ligands with a median
of 2. The components were stratified by whether they contained a cliff pair and by
their mean potency in three bins, and 20 per cent of the components of each stratum
were assigned to the test split. The components remaining in the development split
were distributed over five cross-validation folds by greedy bin packing, taking the
largest components first and assigning each one to the fold with the lowest combined
load of cliff pairs and of ligands.

Balancing on either quantity alone had been tried first and failed in both directions.
Assigning components in rotation produced folds holding 25 cliff pairs against 4, which
leaves a cliff metric computed per fold too noisy to interpret, while balancing the
number of cliff pairs alone produced folds of 13 ligands against 63, because the dense
cliff clusters are small while the large components contain no cliff at all. The joint
criterion gave a development split of 217 ligands and 145 pairs and a test split of 54
ligands and 38 pairs, of which 20 are cliff pairs and 18 are controls, with the five
folds holding between 40 and 45 ligands, between 15 and 17 cliff pairs and between 13
and 14 control pairs. No pair has its two members on opposite sides of the test
boundary or of any fold boundary, which was verified on the written splits rather than
assumed from the construction.

The test split was sealed, i.e. it was to be scored only after a single descriptor set
and a single algorithm had been selected on the development split, since with several
descriptor sets and six algorithm families the number of comparisons is large enough
that a winner can be selected on noise alone. No model reported in this work has been
scored on it. The condition for opening it was the selection of one descriptor set to
carry forward, that condition was not met, and the split therefore remains unused and
available to a later study.

\subsection{Three framings of the prediction problem}\label{sec:framings}

    predicts, how one row is built from the descriptor vectors $\mathbf{d}_a$ and
    $\mathbf{d}_b$ of the two ligands of a pair, the metric, and the number of
    algorithm families. The number in parentheses is the count of units available in
    the development split.}
  \label{tab:framings}
  \input{../results/31_framings.tex}
\end{table}

A comparison between descriptor sets has no single answer, because what counts as an
improvement depends on what is being predicted. A descriptor set can predict potency
well and still fail to tell two near-identical molecules apart, and a set that
separates cliff pairs from control pairs need not predict the size of the difference
within a pair. Three framings of the prediction problem were therefore scored for
every descriptor set, on the folds fixed in Section~\ref{sec:splits} and with the same
algorithms, so that the three results differ in the question being asked and in
nothing else, and the three are set side by side in Table~\ref{tab:framings}. Framing~B was designated the primary comparison before any
molecular-dynamics descriptor had been scored, and that designation is recorded in the
version history of the analysis code rather than being asserted after the fact.

In framing~A the unit is the ligand, the target is its pChEMBL value, and the metric
is the coefficient of determination computed over the out-of-fold predictions of the
development ligands. This is ordinary potency prediction, i.e. the task on which the
QSAR literature is calibrated, and it was included for two reasons. A descriptor set
that cannot predict potency at all is not a serious candidate for predicting a
difference in potency, and a result on this task can be placed beside published models
in a way that a pairwise result cannot.

In framing~B the unit is the pair and the target is the signed difference in pChEMBL
between its two members, so that the quantity predicted is the quantity that defines
an activity cliff. Each pair contributes two training rows, formed by taking the
difference of the two descriptor vectors in both orders together with the
corresponding difference in potency, so that the data are antisymmetric and the
arbitrary decision of which member of a pair is written first cannot influence what
the model learns. The metric is again the coefficient of determination, computed over
the out-of-fold predictions of all rows. This framing is the primary one because the
question of this study is whether a descriptor set can distinguish two molecules that
a fingerprint declares identical, and that distinction is the signed difference within
a pair and nothing else.

In framing~C the unit is again the pair, but the label is categorical, i.e. whether
the pair is a cliff or a matched control, and the metric is the area under the
receiver operating characteristic curve. The features of a pair have to be invariant
to the order of its two members here, because whether a pair is a cliff does not
depend on which of its ligands is written first, and the antisymmetric rows of
framing~B would leave the label ambiguous. Each pair was therefore described by the
concatenation of the elementwise sum and the elementwise absolute difference of the
descriptor vectors of its two ligands, which is symmetric under exchange of the two by
construction. Five algorithm families were used in this framing rather than six, since
the partial least squares regression of the other two framings has no classification
counterpart.

A pair is indivisible in both pair framings. The two rows that a pair contributes to
framing~B were assigned to the same fold and were resampled together, and the pair is
also the unit of the interval estimates described in Section~\ref{sec:eval}. Treating
the two rows of a pair as independent observations would count every pair twice and
would narrow every interval by a factor that has nothing to do with the data.

\subsection{Four descriptor sets of increasing computational
cost}\label{sec:rungs}

\begin{table}[tb]
  \centering
  \small
  \caption{\textbf{The four descriptor sets.} For each rung, the input it requires,
    what its descriptors capture, the number of features it was scored with, and the
    cost of producing it for one ligand. The feature counts are the two feature sets
    of rung~1 and, for rung~4, the full set and the set that survives the reliability
    filter. The costs are median measured values for one ligand, given as
    processor time for the first three rungs and as wall-clock time on one GPU for
    the simulation, which are not the same unit and are not comparable beyond their
    order of magnitude.}
  \label{tab:rungs}
  \input{../results/32_rungs.tex}
\end{table}

Four descriptor families were compared, ordered by the cost of producing them, and
each one is referred to below as a rung of that ladder. The first rung needs only the
molecular graph, the second needs a docked pose, the third adds an energy evaluation
on that pose, and the fourth needs a molecular-dynamics trajectory. The ordering is
the point of the comparison, since the question is not whether a more expensive
representation can be built but whether it buys anything, so every rung was scored
with the same algorithms, on the same folds, and in the three framings of
Section~\ref{sec:framings}. A difference between two rungs is therefore a difference
between representations rather than between model families. The four rungs are set out
in Table~\ref{tab:rungs}, which gives the input each one requires, what its
descriptors capture, how many features it carries and what it cost to produce.

Rung~1 is the two-dimensional baseline and uses no three-dimensional information at
all. Two feature sets were carried rather than one. The first is a binary ECFP4
fingerprint of 2{,}048 bits, i.e. the representation that defined the cliffs in
Section~\ref{sec:dataset}, and the second is a count-based ECFP4 fingerprint of the
same width combined with the 217 physicochemical descriptors of RDKit, which gives
2{,}265 features. Both were scored in every framing and the better of the two was
taken, because neither of them dominates the other. Scoring only one would have
handicapped the baseline against which every other rung was measured, and in a
direction that flatters the more expensive rungs.

Rung~2 was computed from the top-ranked docked pose and consists of 34 descriptors.
They were chosen to test the dual-site mechanism described in Section~\ref{sec:site}
rather than to cast a wide net, and they fall into four groups. The first group holds
the distances from the ligand to Trp86, to Trp286 and to the Tyr337 gate, together
with five descriptors of how far the ligand reaches along the axis between the two
sites and what fraction of that axis it spans. The second holds the cation--$\pi$
contacts at each of the two tryptophans and at both of them simultaneously. The third
is a contact count for each of the fourteen gorge-lining residues. The fourth
describes the ligand itself through its radius of gyration, its end-to-end distance,
its buried fraction and its heavy-atom count. Every descriptor is the median over the
three docking seeds, and the spread across seeds was retained for diagnosis but was
not used as a feature. The heavy-atom count is among the features, which is worth
stating plainly, since the two groups of pairs differ in size as reported in
Section~\ref{sec:composition} and this rung is therefore able to use that difference.

The same descriptors were also computed over an ensemble of receptor conformations,
which is reported separately as rung~2e. The rigidity of a single receptor was
identified in Section~\ref{sec:dock} as a limitation of the docking rather than of the
protocol, so eight crystal structures of hAChE, i.e. 4EY4, 4EY7, 4M0F, 6CQX, 6CQY,
6O4X, 6O5V and 6WUY, were selected by clustering on the side-chain positions of the
gorge-lining residues after superposition. Selecting on conformational difference
rather than on resolution or on date of deposition is what makes an ensemble useful,
since the best-resolved structures would most likely have returned several copies of
the same conformation. Each ligand was docked into all eight receptors and each
descriptor was reduced to its median across them, which gives 29 descriptors, i.e.
those of rung~2 without the five axis descriptors, which are defined against the
single 4EY7 geometry.

Rung~3 evaluated an MM/GBSA interaction energy on the same docked pose and consists of
22 features, i.e. the total together with its molecular-mechanics, polar-solvation and
non-polar components, the buried surface area, the interface atom counts, and a
per-residue decomposition over the same fourteen residues. The calculation is
single-trajectory, i.e. the complex, the protein and the ligand were taken from the same
coordinates and nothing is re-minimised after separation, so that the internal bonded
terms cancel exactly and only the intermolecular interaction and the desolvation
remain. The receptor was rigid during docking, so the protein term is identical for
every ligand and was computed once. This is not a rigorous binding free energy and is
not presented as one, since absolute values obtained from a single post-docking
snapshot are not defensible. It is on the ladder because it is what a structure-based
study routinely does at this cost, and the question of this work is whether that
routine step buys anything.

Rung~4 was derived from molecular-dynamics trajectories, which are described in
Section~\ref{sec:md}, and the descriptors themselves are defined in
Section~\ref{sec:mddesc}. Thirty-three descriptors were computed, covering how
persistently the ligand contacts each end of the gorge, where it sits on the axis
between the two ends, how the Tyr337 gate behaves while it is bound, and how much the
ligand and the surrounding residues fluctuate. Twelve of the thirty-three survived the
reliability filter described in Section~\ref{sec:filter}, which removes descriptors
whose variation between repeated simulations of the same ligand is comparable to their
variation between ligands. Both the full set and the filtered set were scored, since
reporting only one of them would leave open whether a result is a property of the
descriptors or of the filter.

The number of features is not matched across the rungs, since the two-dimensional sets
carry 2{,}048 and 2{,}265 features against between 12 and 34 for every structure-based
set. This is deliberate, because each rung was scored in the form in which it would
actually be used, and a comparison that first cut a fingerprint down to 34 features
would not describe what any practitioner does. The consequence is that a difference
between two rungs is a difference between representations at their natural
dimensionality rather than a controlled test of how much information each one carries.
A matched-dimensionality arm was therefore scored as well, with the feature selection
performed inside each training fold so that the held-out fold can never influence which
features are chosen, which is the most common way such a comparison is made to look
fair while being anything but.
