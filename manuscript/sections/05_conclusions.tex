\section{Conclusions}

This study measures what escalating physical detail buys on activity cliffs, with a
ligand-based baseline scored on the same frozen splits and a detection limit fixed
before anything had been compared, which is the combination the existing evidence on
this question lacks. Across 145 matched pairs of human acetylcholinesterase, 77 of them
activity cliffs, and three framings of the endpoint, none of the four structure-based
representations is resolvably better than a two-dimensional fingerprint, and on
per-ligand potency the molecular dynamics descriptors are resolvably worse. That is a
result with a direction rather than an absence of one. The two readings that would make
it uninteresting are excluded by measurement rather than by argument, since the
variance the ladder leaves unexplained is far beyond what assay reproducibility can
account for, and the scores do not move with trajectory length once the descriptor set
is held fixed.

Three findings transfer beyond this target. A \SI{25}{\nano\second} trajectory
reproduces how long a contact is held, in 53 of 100 combinations of descriptor and
analysis window, while it reproduces the amplitude of motion in 3 of 35 and the rate of
discrete events in 4 of 20. Persistence is therefore what a trajectory of this length
can be asked for, and amplitude largely is not. A descriptor filter refitted at every
trajectory length manufactures a rise that disappears once a single selection is
carried across the windows, which exposes any study that re-selects features at each
value of a parameter it is varying. The descriptors that survive the reproducibility
criterion are available from \SI{15}{\nano\second} of simulation rather
than the 50 the original design called for, which answers the cost objection with a
measurement rather than an argument. What this design cannot see is a modest advantage,
and the quantity that would settle that is the number of pairs and of targets rather
than the nanoseconds spent on each.
