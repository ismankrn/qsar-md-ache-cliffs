Each descriptor is named here as it appears in the deposited descriptor tables, so a
quantity discussed in the text can be traced to the column it was computed in.
Rungs~2, 2e and~4 share one panel of fourteen gorge-lining residues and compute a
per-residue quantity for each of them, which is why three of these tables end with a
block of fourteen entries. What that quantity is differs between the three, being a
contact count on one docked pose, the same count taken as a median over eight receptor
conformations, and the fraction of simulated frames in which the contact was present.
The residue numbering is that of the mature human enzyme as deposited in 4EY7, and the
names were read from the receptor used in the calculations rather than assigned by hand.
The \SI{4.5}{\angstrom} cutoff that defines a contact is the same at all three rungs,
so a per-residue entry means the same geometric thing wherever it appears.

Five of the rung-2 descriptors measure the ligand's position along the axis between the
two anionic sites, and they are defined against the single 4EY7 geometry, so the
ensemble carries the remaining 29 and not those five. The rung-4 table carries two
further columns, namely the spread of a descriptor between ligands divided by its spread
between replicate simulations of one ligand, and the verdict that ratio produced at a
threshold of two. A descriptor whose replicate spread was zero has no ratio, and that
case splits in two, as a quantity that never varies at all carries no information while
one that is almost always zero is reproducible only by its own sparsity. The heavy-atom
count appears in the same table because it is carried beside either feature set as a
size covariate, and it is not a dynamic quantity and was not put to the filter.
Section~\ref{sec:md} gives the pilot the filter was computed from and
Section~\ref{sec:rungs} the counts that survived it, neither of which names a
descriptor.

Tables~\ref{tab:benchreg} and~\ref{tab:benchclf} hold the full grid of scores that the
ladder summarises, with every rung crossed against every descriptor arm and every
algorithm family. The headline comparison takes the best value a rung reached over its
arms and its families, which is one number drawn from a block of them, and these two
tables are that block. They are split because the regression and the classification
framings do not share a model suite, as Ridge and PLS have no classification counterpart
and are absent from the second rather than having failed in it. Reading down a column
shows how much of a rung's score is the descriptors and how much is the choice of
algorithm, which the single best value conceals. The arms are the alternative feature
sets of Section~\ref{sec:rungs}, namely the two fingerprint variants of rung~1 and the
filtered and unfiltered sets of rung~4, while rungs~2, 2e and~3 carry one arm each.
