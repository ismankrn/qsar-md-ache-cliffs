The six targets screened differed sharply in the kind of cliff they offered, which is
shown in Figure~\ref{fig:dataset}(a), where the cliff pairs of each target are divided
into constitutional and stereochemical ones. BACE1 returned 101 stereochemical pairs against 5 constitutional ones, and the three
other rejected targets returned between 9 and 17 constitutional pairs. Four of the six
therefore fell below the entry criterion of 25 constitutional cliffs, although all six
satisfied the criteria on compound count and on potency range. Acetylcholinesterase inverted that ratio at 100 constitutional pairs
against 10 stereochemical ones, and coagulation factor Xa was the only other target to
pass, at 33 against 16. Every pair that reached the final set is constitutional, since the two members differ
in molecular formula in all 97 pairs and the smallest difference in heavy-atom count
is one. No result reported below therefore rests on a pair that a stereo-blind
fingerprint is barred from resolving by construction.

The fingerprint also fails to separate pairs that did enter the set, which is shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:dataset}(b) for all 97 cliff pairs. Binary ECFP4 reported a Tanimoto coefficient of exactly 1.000 for 49 of the 97. The
two members of those pairs differ by a median of 4 and by as many as 8 heavy atoms,
and they differ in potency by a median of 2.51 log units. Count-based ECFP4, which retains how often each substructure occurs rather than only
whether it occurs at all, separated the same 49 pairs at a median coefficient of
0.878. Folding counts to bits therefore discards exactly the multiplicity that
distinguishes a molecule from its longer homologue. This is not only
a statement about the descriptors, because the criterion that selected these pairs is
the binary fingerprint itself. The similarity recorded at selection was compared with the audited binary coefficient
over all 97 pairs, and the two agree exactly, at a maximum absolute difference of
zero. The measure which chose the pairs is therefore the measure that cannot tell
their two members apart. A model given that representation
alone has no feature on which to place the two members differently, whatever algorithm
is fitted on top of it.

The consequences of the definition for the selected set are shown in the lower two
panels. Figure~\ref{fig:dataset}(c) places the pairs in the plane of the definition itself,
with similarity on the axis measured by the binary fingerprint that performed the
selection. The empty band between the cliff pairs above 2.0 log units and the control
pairs below 0.30 is imposed by the criterion and is not a property of the chemistry. Figure~\ref{fig:dataset}(d) shows that the two groups also differ in size, since the
two members of a cliff pair differ by 4.4 heavy atoms on average against 1.8 for a
control pair. Any descriptor correlated with molecular size therefore separates the
groups for a reason that has nothing to do with the cliff. The 19 pose-geometry and energy descriptors were therefore tested again with
that difference adjusted for, and although 4 of them separated the two groups in a raw
test, none of the 19 survived either a stratified or a residual adjustment.

The two limitations above act differently on a comparison of this kind, and they are
worth keeping apart for that reason. The stereochemical one favours descriptors
derived from three-dimensional structure, since
a stereoisomer pair is unresolvable by a stereo-blind fingerprint and trivial for any
method that uses coordinates, and it is the reason that four of the six candidate
targets were rejected. The saturation of the binary fingerprint favours no method in
particular and instead corrupts the selection of the pairs themselves. Neither is specific to this target or to molecular dynamics, since both follow from a
default molecular representation. Both are therefore inherited by any activity-cliff
benchmark assembled in the usual way, which is why they are reported here rather than
treated as a local difficulty.

\subsection{Docking reproduces the reference pose and its
contacts}\label{sec:dockval}

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.62\textwidth]{Figure2_redocking_validation.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Redocking validation.} Donepezil redocked into 4EY7, its own
    structure, against the crystal pose, with Trp86 and Tyr337 marked.}
  \label{fig:redock}
\end{figure}

We validated the protocol against four ligands whose complexes with hAChE have been
solved, since a docked pose can only be scored against a crystallographic answer and
no ligand of the dataset has one. They were chosen to span different modes of binding, so that the validation would not
rest on ligands that resemble one another. Donepezil comes from 4EY7 and spans both
ends of the gorge, territrem~B from 4M0F is large at 527~Da, and
huperzine~A from 4EY5 and galantamine from 4EY6 occupy the catalytic site alone. Each was docked into
4EY7 with five seeds, and a pose was counted as successful below \SI{2.0}{\angstrom}
RMSD from the crystal pose. Donepezil, the ligand of 4EY7 itself, was redocked at
$0.745 \pm 0.009$~\AA{} in five of five runs, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:redock}, and
territrem~B was cross-docked at $0.740 \pm 0.012$~\AA{} in five of five. The two
remaining cross-dockings did not succeed, since galantamine gave $2.463 \pm
1.709$~\AA{} in four of five runs and huperzine~A gave $3.098 \pm 0.011$~\AA{} in none
of them. The failure of huperzine~A
is systematic and not stochastic, as the spread across seeds is \SI{0.011}{\angstrom},
and the size of the search box was ruled out as its cause, since repeating the docking
from 18 to \SI{30}{\angstrom} per side left the RMSD unchanged. What remains is that
one receptor conformation cannot accommodate every binding mode, which is the reason
for the ensemble docking of Section~\ref{sec:rungs} and, ultimately, for the molecular
dynamics of Section~\ref{sec:md}. Vina also proved strongly stochastic for small
ligands, since galantamine gave 5.88 and \SI{1.60}{\angstrom} on different seeds under
otherwise identical settings, which is why we fixed the seeds and ran three replicates
for every ligand.

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_interactions.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{The reference binding mode, and docking checked at the level of
    contacts.} (a)~Donepezil in the crystal pose with the residues it contacts,
    where the colour of each edge gives the type of interaction. (b)~The same
    fingerprint calculated for the redocked pose.}
  \label{fig:interactions}
\end{figure}

The pose-geometry and molecular-dynamics descriptors are defined in terms of residue
contacts and not of RMSD, so we validated the protocol a second time at the level of
interactions. Interaction fingerprints were computed for the crystal
pose and the redocked pose of donepezil by using ProLIF \citep{prolif2021}, with bond orders
transferred from the docked structure so that the two poses carry identical chemistry
and differ only in their coordinates. The crystal binding mode is shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:interactions}(a) and the comparison between the two fingerprints in
Figure~\ref{fig:interactions}(b). Six residues are contacted in both poses, five in the crystal pose only and two in the
docked pose only. Every typed interaction at the three named sites is recovered,
namely the hydrophobic and $\pi$-stacking contacts at Trp86 and at Trp286 and the
cation--$\pi$ and hydrophobic contacts at Tyr337, while what is lost on docking is van
der Waals contact alone with Ser203 and His447. We used the typing for validation only, and it enters no descriptor set.

\label{sec:synthesis}

\NOTE{(1) Five descriptor rungs on three endpoints, one target, 145 pairs, with a
  resolution of about 0.30 in $R^2$ stated in advance and holding. (2) No
