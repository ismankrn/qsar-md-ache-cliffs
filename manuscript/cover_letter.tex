\noindent
To the Editors, \emph{Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling}

\medskip
\noindent
Dear Editors,

We submit for your consideration an Article entitled \textbf{``Activity cliffs on human
acetylcholinesterase: a pre-specified descriptor escalation''}.

The standard explanation for why two-dimensional fingerprints fail on activity cliffs
is that they cannot see the detail separating the two members of a pair. That
explanation has gone untested, because the two lines of evidence have never met on the
same compounds and neither reports what difference its design could have detected. What
this paper offers is that test and that bound, not a new descriptor. We escalated four structure-based representations against
the same fingerprint baseline on the same frozen splits, and we measured in advance
what difference the design could resolve. The dynamics rung required a trajectory for
every ligand rather than for a representative few, 283 production runs and 8{,}319~ns
in all, which is what lets it meet the fingerprint on the same ligands and pairs as
every other rung. On the development split none was resolvably
better on any of three framings of the endpoint, and on per-ligand potency the
molecular dynamics descriptors were resolvably worse. The result is bounded rather than open, which is what separates it
from an underpowered null.

We also opened the sealed test split, once, after every descriptor set and algorithm
had been fixed, and it does not reproduce that result. On the signed pair difference the
receptor ensemble and the dynamics descriptors both lead the fingerprints there by
about 0.3 in the coefficient of determination, and both gaps survive a correction for
the twelve comparisons made and a leave-one-pair-out check. We report the disagreement
rather than either half of it, because 38 held-out pairs against 145 development pairs
cannot settle which reading is right. We would rather the Editors meet it here than
find it in Section~3.5.

Three findings carry beyond this target. First, a replicate test separates persistence
from amplitude, so an affordable trajectory reproduces how long a contact is held and
not how far things move. Second, a descriptor filter refitted at
each value of a varied parameter manufactures a trend that a single fixed selection
removes, which exposes any study that re-selects features as it varies one.
Third, the descriptors that survive reproducibility are available from 15 ns of
simulation rather than the 50 the original design called for.

We would like to address three points of the Journal's guidance on QSAR submissions
before a reader reaches them. First, this is not a docking study against a single
target: the docking serves a comparison between representations, and what is under test
is what physical detail adds to a matched ligand-based baseline. Second, the high
ratio of descriptors to data points sits in the fingerprint baseline, scored as a
practitioner would score it. We report a matched-dimensionality arm alongside it so
that feature count can be separated from representation. Third, as above, we propose
no new descriptors and claim no superiority for any; the machinery is standard
throughout and attributed as such.

We chose acetylcholinesterase because the question needs cliffs in quantity and a
crystallographic human receptor, and because its gorge gates conformationally on
evidence predating this work, which makes it favourable to the hypothesis under test.

All molecular structures appear in the Supporting Information, and all analysis code
and derived data are in a public repository archived with a DOI, which reproduces every
number without rerunning a simulation.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has been approved
by all authors. The authors declare no competing financial interest, and the work
received no external funding.
